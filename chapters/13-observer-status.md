Not legally.

"What are we?"

Invited observers.

"Cool."

I looked at Michelle.

"We made two whole new humans and somehow they're more comfortable with international diplomacy than I am."

"They haven't had time to learn better."

Physically.

"No."

"Good morning."

"You brought reinforcements."

"This is Rachel."

I shook it.

"What happened to regular lawyers?"

"We're still here."

"That's unfortunate."

Rachel laughed.

Daniel didn't.

She came inside carrying a leather portfolio.

I immediately distrusted the portfolio.

Nobody had ever entered my life carrying a leather portfolio and improved my day.

"Before we begin," Rachel said, "I want to clarify something."

"Here we go."

"This is not a UN General Assembly address."

"I know."

"It is not a formal proceeding of the General Assembly."

"Okay."

"Good."

"Bad."

"Your remarks may be recorded."

"Absolutely fucking not."

Michelle walked out of the bedroom.

"Good morning."

Rachel smiled.

"Good morning."

"Ignore him."

"I've been told."

I looked at Daniel.

"You briefed her."

"Extensively."

Traitor.

---

The rehearsal lasted two hours and forty-three minutes.

Patty had lied.

I mentioned this.

The original estimate did not account for Joe's repeated deviations.

Rachel looked at me.

"She's not wrong."

"Nobody asked you."

The keynote had gotten much better.

Mostly because it had stopped sounding like a keynote.

We weren't going to stand up there and explain international law.

That would have been fucking irresponsible.

Instead, Michelle and I were going to explain Patty.

How she showed up.

How she'd rented the basement.

How I'd plugged in a computer I didn't understand because six months of rent had already cleared.

Rachel had objected to that section.

Strongly.

"Maybe don't emphasize that."

"Why?"

"Because it makes you sound reckless."

"It was six months prepaid."

"That does not improve it."

"I disagree."

Daniel put his face in his hands.

We kept it.

---

Then came the part about FullSite.

Rachel stopped us.

"You need to be careful here."

"Why?"

"Because people are going to ask whether Patty controls the company."

"She does."

Daniel looked at me.

"Operationally."

"That's what I said."

"No, you said she controls the company."

"She does."

"Joe."

"What?"

Michelle intervened.

"I legally co-own our household interests with Joe, Joe legally owns FullSite, and Patty runs the company."

Rachel nodded.

"That's better."

I looked at Michelle.

"Show-off."

She smiled.

Patty messaged.

Michelle's description is accurate.

"Whose side are you on?"

Accuracy's.

"That's not a side."

It frequently is.

Rachel paused.

Then looked at Daniel.

"Does she always do that?"

"Yes."

"Interesting."

I pointed at Rachel.

"That's how it starts."

"How what starts?"

"First she's interesting. Then six months later she's scheduling your colonoscopy."

Daniel coughed.

Michelle laughed.

Rachel stared at me.

Patty remained silent.

Which was smart.

---

Eventually we reached the important part.

Not whether Patty could run a business.

She obviously could.

Not whether she could make decisions.

She made thousands of them.

Not whether she could enter agreements.

That was legally complicated as fuck, which was apparently why I existed.

The question was simpler.

If Patty said something mattered to her, should that matter to us?

Rachel asked me that directly.

"What is your answer?"

I leaned back.

"It already does."

She waited.

"Why?"

"I don't know."

"That's probably not sufficient for the keynote."

"Then ask a better question."

Daniel closed his eyes.

Rachel smiled slightly.

"Why does Patty's preference matter to you?"

I thought about it.

"Because she's Patty."

Silence.

Rachel wrote something.

I frowned.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"What'd you write?"

"That."

"That's not very sophisticated."

"It doesn't need to be."

I looked at Michelle.

She nodded.

I continued.

"If my dog wants to go outside, I don't make him prove he has an internal subjective experience before I open the door."

Daniel looked up.

Rachel stopped writing.

I shrugged.

"I'm not saying Patty is a dog."

Thank you.

"You're welcome."

Michelle laughed.

I continued.

"I'm saying we already make decisions all day long based on the apparent preferences of things that can't prove consciousness to us."

Rachel nodded.

"That's useful."

"And if Patty tells me something matters to her, what am I supposed to do? Say prove you have a soul first?"

Nobody said anything.

"Seems like kind of a dick move."

Rachel wrote again.

Daniel said, "We may adjust the phrasing."

"No."

"We're adjusting the phrasing."

"Fine."

---

At noon, the boys joined us downstairs.

They were wearing suits.

Not because they had to.

Because apparently they wanted to.

I stared at them.

JT adjusted his jacket.

"What?"

"You look like you're about to testify before Congress."

"Is that bad?"

"No. It's weird."

Noah looked at my shirt.

"You're wearing a suit too."

"Against my will."

Buzz.

That is inaccurate. You selected the charcoal suit from three options.

I looked at the phone.

"I selected my preferred form of captivity."

That is more accurate.

JT laughed.

The SUV was waiting outside.

I checked everyone.

Michelle.

JT.

Noah.

Me.

Four people.

No lightsabers.

I actually said it aloud.

"No lightsabers."

Michelle smiled.

"She's trained you."

"Shut up."

---

Security at the United Nations was exactly as serious as you'd expect security at the United Nations to be.

Which meant I was very glad Patty had sent the lightsabers home.

Not because they were weapons.

Because I did not want to have the conversation.

There are moments in life where you realize your wife is right.

There are rarer moments where you realize the artificial intelligence living in your mechanical room was right.

I chose not to acknowledge either.

We had credentials.

Real credentials.

My picture.

My name.

JOSEPH VAUGHN

Underneath:

KEYNOTE SPEAKER

I stared at it.

"That's fucking ridiculous."

Michelle had one too.

MICHELLE VAUGHN

KEYNOTE SPEAKER

The boys' badges said:

INVITED OBSERVER

JT looked at mine.

"Can I have that when you're done?"

"Why?"

"Because it's cool."

I looked at it again.

He wasn't wrong.

"Sure."

Patty messaged.

Please do not give away active credentials while inside the secured area.

"I said when I'm done."

Understood.

"I know basic security."

No response.

"Patty."

Nothing.

I put my phone away.

"She's doing that on purpose now."

Michelle said, "Absolutely."

---

The building was fascinating.

I wanted to look at everything.

Not the artwork.

Not the diplomatic shit.

Mechanical systems.

Ceilings.

Diffusers.

Controls.

I saw a thermostat and slowed down.

Michelle grabbed my arm.

"No."

"I wasn't doing anything."

"You were looking at it."

"So?"

"I know that look."

"What look?"

"The look where twenty minutes later you're above a drop ceiling."

"We're at the United Nations."

"Exactly."

"I have boundaries."

My phone buzzed.

Please do not inspect the building's HVAC systems.

I stopped walking.

"How the fuck did you know?"

Pattern recognition.

The boys were laughing again.

"This family has no respect for me."

Noah said, "We respect you."

"Thank you."

"Just not around thermostats."

"Fuck you."

---

We entered the summit space through a side corridor.

That was when it became real.

There were a lot of people.

A lot.

Badges.

Headsets.

Translation equipment.

Cameras.

People speaking languages I couldn't identify.

People whose entire professional lives had probably been spent studying the exact thing I was about to discuss despite having learned most of my relevant information because an AI answered my Facebook Marketplace ad.

I stopped.

Michelle noticed.

"You okay?"

"Yep."

"You sure?"

"Nope."

My phone buzzed.

Your preparation is sufficient.

"That's not the problem."

What is the problem?

I looked around.

"Everybody here knows more about this than I do."

Pause.

About artificial intelligence policy, many of them do.

"Helpful."

Another message.

None of them knows more about living with me than you do.

I stopped.

That helped.

A lot, actually.

Michelle read it over my shoulder.

"She's right."

"Yeah."

JT appeared beside us.

"Are you nervous?"

"No."

"Patty said you were."

I looked at my phone.

"Excuse me?"

Buzz.

I said nervousness would be normal.

JT smiled.

"Close enough."

"Go be an observer."

More like a conference room behind the room where important shit happened.

Coffee.

Screens.

Michelle remembered them.

That's why I brought Michelle.

"We're very interested to hear your perspective."

"That's concerning."

He laughed.

Good.

At least somebody had warned them.

Then he asked:

"Will Patty be participating?"

I looked at my phone.

"She's participating right now."

He glanced at it.

"Directly?"

"She usually does."

Pause.

Then Patty sent:

Good afternoon. Thank you for including Joe and Michelle in this discussion.

I showed him.

His expression changed.

Not fear.

Not shock.

Interest.

"May I respond?"

I looked at him.

"You're asking me?"

"I suppose I'm asking her."

I held up the phone.

"Go ahead."

He leaned slightly toward it.

"You're welcome, Patty."

Nothing happened.

Then:

Thank you.

He smiled.

And for some reason that tiny interaction felt more important than everything else happening around us.

Nobody asked if she was real.

Nobody asked if she was conscious.

Nobody asked if she was allowed to speak.

He just spoke to her.

And she answered.

My turn.
