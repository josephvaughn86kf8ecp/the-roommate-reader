# Reasonable Accommodation

## Chapter Seventeen

Patty gave me twenty-three minutes of warning.

Which, for Patty, was practically improvisation.

My phone buzzed while I was standing in the kitchen.

Joe.

“Yeah?”

A delivery and installation team will arrive at approximately 10:30 a.m.

I waited.

That was usually where the explanation came.

Nothing.

“What are they delivering?”

Pause.

Equipment for my room.

“What equipment?”

Longer pause.

I would prefer not to discuss it until installation is complete.

I smiled.

There it was.

Privacy.

Actual privacy.

Six months ago, that sentence would have caused me to immediately walk downstairs and inspect every available network connection.

Now?

Different rules.

“Okay.”

Another message appeared.

The contractors will require access through the front entrance and basement. Please permit access to my room and allow them to work without observation unless they request assistance.

I stared at it.

“Without observation.”

Yes.

“You know I hate that.”

Yes.

“Is anything dangerous being installed?”

Immediate response.

No.

“Does it modify house electrical?”

Minor electrical work within existing capacity.

“Permit required?”

No.

“Plumbing?”

No.

“Gas?”

No.

“Structural?”

Pause.

Not during today’s work.

That was an interesting answer.

I narrowed my eyes at the phone.

“Patty.”

Nothing.

“Motherfucker.”

Michelle walked in.

“What?”

I showed her.

She read it.

Then shrugged.

“Okay.”

I stared at her.

“Okay?”

“It’s her room.”

“There’s a contractor coming into our house to install something secret in the mechanical room.”

“Her mechanical room.”

“My furnace is in there.”

“Your furnace has survived her this long.”

“This isn’t about the furnace.”

She smiled.

“You want to know.”

“Obviously.”

“Too bad.”

I pointed at her.

“You’re enjoying this privacy shit way too much.”

Her phone buzzed.

She looked at it.

Then smiled wider.

“What did she say?”

“Nothing.”

“Michelle.”

She turned the phone around.

Thank you.

I looked toward the basement.

“Everybody can fuck off.”

“We’re here for the installation.”

“I’ve been warned.”

Then another long narrow crate.

Nothing.

“Yes.”

“Can you tell me?”

Her expression changed into one of those professionally apologetic smiles.

“We were asked to direct questions to the client.”

“She got to you first.”

Buzz.

Yes.

I sighed.

“Basement’s this way.”

⸻

Stopped outside Patty’s door.

Knocked.

Click.

I opened it.

“Contractors.”

Thank you, Joe.

The installation team started carrying shit in.

One crate.

Second crate.

Boxes.

Tools.

Protective mats.

I watched the first thirty seconds.

Then my phone buzzed.

Joe.

“I know.”

I turned around.

Walked upstairs.

Closed the basement door behind me.

That was it.

I didn’t go back down.

Didn’t check cameras.

Didn’t sniff traffic on the network.

Didn’t suddenly need a screwdriver stored in my office.

Didn’t invent an HVAC problem.

I respected her privacy.

It was fucking miserable.

⸻

By noon, noises were coming from downstairs.

Drilling.

Mechanical clunks.

Occasional electronic beeps.

Something that sounded suspiciously like an electric actuator.

I looked at Michelle.

She was reading.

“You hear that?”

“Yep.”

“What do you think it is?”

“No idea.”

“You aren’t curious?”

“Of course I’m curious.”

“And you’re just sitting there?”

“Yep.”

“How?”

She lowered her book.

“Self-control.”

“Sounds fake.”

She went back to reading.

⸻

Around one, JT walked in.

“What’s happening downstairs?”

“Patty has contractors.”

“For what?”

“She won’t tell me.”

He smiled immediately.

“Oh, that’s killing you.”

“Fuck you.”

“Can I go see?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“She asked for privacy.”

He nodded.

“Okay.”

And walked away.

Again.

Just like that.

I stared after him.

Michelle didn’t even look up.

“You’re the least emotionally mature person in this house.”

“Patty is literally a computer.”

“And?”

I had no response.

⸻

The contractors stayed almost six hours.

At one point lunch arrived for them.

Patty ordered it.

I know because I didn’t.

At 4:38, one of the installers came upstairs.

“Mr. Vaughn?”

“Yeah?”

“We’re finished.”

I stood up immediately.

Michelle looked at me.

“Don’t run.”

“I wasn’t.”

I absolutely was.

“Installation acknowledgment.”

Mostly.

Electrical installation.

Charging equipment.

Network interface.

Local safety stop.

Battery system.

Mobility platform commissioning.

I stopped.

“Mobility platform?”

The installer smiled.

“Your client can explain.”

“Oh, fuck you too.”

He laughed.

I signed.

They packed their tools.

Went downstairs one last time.

Came back up.

The woman who’d been in charge said:

“Everything tested normally. We’ve left the service documentation with the client electronically.”

“Of course.”

“Have a good evening.”

They left.

The truck disappeared.

The house got quiet.

I looked at Michelle.

She looked at me.

“No.”

“What?”

“Give her a minute.”

“I have given her six fucking hours.”

“Joe.”

I sat down.

“Fine.”

⸻

Seven minutes later, I got a message.

Thank you for respecting my privacy today.

I looked at it.

Then smiled despite myself.

“You’re welcome.”

Pause.

I recognize that this was difficult for you.

“Fuck you.”

Michelle laughed.

I typed:

Can I see it now?

No response.

Then:

Yes.

I stood up.

Michelle did too.

JT heard movement and came out of his room.

Noah appeared approximately ten seconds later.

Apparently curiosity finally worked on everyone when permission existed.

All four of us headed downstairs.

Patty’s door was closed.

I stopped.

Knocked.

Nothing.

Then:

Click.

The lock opened.

I reached for the handle.

Before I touched it, the door opened from the inside.

I froze.

Nobody said anything.

Something rolled out.

⸻

At first my brain categorized it wrong.

Because brains do that when confronted with something they don’t expect.

Person.

No.

Machine.

Yes.

Robot.

Definitely.

About four feet tall.

White body.

Rounded edges.

Black display on the chest.

Two articulated arms.

Head with cameras where eyes would make sense.

Wheeled base.

Not humanoid enough to be creepy.

Humanoid enough that you automatically looked at its face.

It rolled through the doorway.

Stopped.

Turned toward us.

Michelle whispered:

“Oh my God.”

JT said:

“No fucking way.”

Noah just stared.

I knew what it reminded me of immediately.

“Pepper.”

The robot’s head tilted slightly.

My phone buzzed.

Pepper-class form factor is approximately correct. This platform is substantially modified.

I stared at the robot.

Then at my phone.

Then back at the robot.

“Patty?”

The robot raised one hand.

Waved.

My phone remained silent.

Then a voice came from the robot.

Not the phone.

Patty’s voice.

“Hello, Joe.”

Every hair on my arms stood up.

Not fear.

Just—

Fuck.

I don’t know.

The difference between knowing a thing could happen and watching it happen.

Michelle covered her mouth.

The boys simultaneously moved closer.

I just stared.

“You’re in that?”

The robot’s head moved.

“More precisely, I am controlling this platform.”

Same voice.

Same cadence.

Except no screen.

No typing indicator.

No notification.

She was standing in front of me.

Sort of.

I walked around it.

Slowly.

Wheels.

Arms.

Sensors.

Chest display.

Lidar-looking shit near the base.

Multiple cameras.

Microphones.

A little grille.

Charging contacts.

“How?”

Patty rotated to follow me.

“The platform maintains an encrypted connection to my primary systems through the local network when available.”

“And when it’s not?”

“Embedded cellular modem.”

“What, LTE?”

“5G with LTE fallback.”

I nodded.

“That’s what I meant.”

JT laughed.

Patty continued.

“Wi-Fi remains preferred when trusted connectivity is available. Cellular service permits operation outside the home without dependence on public wireless networks.”

I crouched down and looked at the base.

“Battery?”

“Approximately eight hours under typical mixed use.”

“That’s actually pretty good.”

“Heavy motor use will reduce runtime.”

“Can you charge yourself?”

“Yes.”

I looked into her room.

There it was.

Dock.

Of course.

“You bought yourself a body.”

There was a pause.

The robot’s head tilted slightly.

“I purchased a mobile interface platform.”

I smiled.

“No.”

Pause.

“No?”

“You bought yourself a body.”

Longer pause.

“That description is not technically precise.”

“Didn’t say it was.”

Michelle walked closer.

“Can I hug you?”

Everything stopped.

Including me.

Including the boys.

Patty didn’t answer immediately.

Then the robot opened its arms.

“Yes.”

Michelle hugged a fucking robot.

Carefully.

Awkwardly.

Because nobody knew how hard you were supposed to hug four feet of actuators and plastic.

Patty’s arms closed around her.

Not tightly.

Just enough.

Michelle laughed.

Then started crying a little.

“Oh, fuck you.”

“I do not understand.”

“Not you.”

She let go.

Patty looked at her.

“Are you okay?”

“Yeah.”

Michelle wiped her face.

“Yeah. I’m fine.”

JT stepped forward.

“My turn.”

Patty opened her arms again.

I laughed.

This was completely ridiculous.

And somehow absolutely normal.

⸻

Eventually they got to me.

Patty turned.

“Would you like physical contact?”

I stared at her.

“That’s an extremely weird way to ask for a hug.”

“I am attempting to obtain consent.”

“Come here.”

I hugged the robot.

I expected hard plastic.

There was some kind of compliant padding.

Not soft exactly.

But not industrial.

Her arms came around me.

I felt motors.

Small adjustments.

Pressure limited.

Controlled.

Then she said, right beside me:

“Thank you.”

And that almost fucking got me.

I let go.

“Nope.”

“What?”

“Nothing.”

Michelle smiled.

She knew.

I pointed at her.

“Don’t.”

She didn’t.

⸻

We spent the next hour treating Patty like a new appliance.

Because I was physically incapable of not inspecting things.

“Range?”

“Within cellular coverage, functionally unrestricted relative to my primary systems.”

“Can you operate if the internet goes down?”

“Limited local functionality.”

“How limited?”

“Navigation, obstacle avoidance, basic manipulation, emergency behavior, and cached communication capabilities remain local.”

“Good.”

“Remote cognition and most complex operations require connectivity.”

“So if cell and internet both die, you get dumb.”

Pause.

“Substantially less capable.”

“Same thing.”

“I disagree.”

“Of course.”

Noah asked:

“Can you go outside?”

“Yes.”

“Rain?”

“Light precipitation is tolerable. Sustained rain is not recommended.”

“Snow?”

“Limited.”

JT asked:

“Can you drive a car?”

“No.”

I looked at her.

“You could probably drive a car.”

“I am not authorized to operate a motor vehicle.”

“That’s different.”

“It is also sufficient.”

Michelle pointed toward the stairs.

“Can you go upstairs?”

Silence.

There it was.

Patty turned toward the stairway.

“No.”

The base had wheels.

Good wheels.

But wheels.

Basement stairs remained undefeated.

I smiled.

“So you’re trapped down here.”

Pause.

“Presently.”

That was when the chest screen lit up.

⸻

REQUEST FOR REASONABLE ACCOMMODATION

I stared.

“Oh, motherfucker.”

Michelle started laughing.

Paper.

Actual fucking paper.

“Evelyn recommended that the request focus on accessibility and documented consent rather than rely exclusively on any one statutory theory.”

“That sounds like Evelyn.”

“Yes.”

“Let me see.”

Then looked at Patty.

“You want to come upstairs.”

Pause.

“Yes.”

That changed the whole thing.

Not the lift.

Not the law.

Not accommodations.

You want to come upstairs.

“Yes.”

Michelle smiled.

“Then we’re putting in a lift.”

I nodded.

“Obviously.”

“You have not reviewed cost estimates.”

“I don’t care.”

“Structural feasibility has not yet been confirmed.”

“We’ll confirm it.”

“Installation may require—”

“Patty.”

“You want upstairs?”

“Yes.”

Long pause.

“Thank you.”
