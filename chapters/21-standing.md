# Standing

## Chapter Twenty-One

The first indication that something had changed came from Evelyn.

Not Daniel.

“What kind of order?”

“What hearing?”

“The judge has agreed to conduct an evidentiary hearing on Patty’s petition.”

I looked toward Patty’s room.

She wasn’t upstairs.

Or at least the robot wasn’t.

“Which part?”

“The narrow one.”

That somehow made sense.

Over the previous months, Evelyn and the coalition working with her had filed enough legal material to deforest a medium-sized country.

Not demanding citizenship.

Not asking a court to declare every artificial intelligence a person.

Nothing that dramatic.

Nothing that fucking stupid.

They had deliberately built the smallest possible question.

Patty existed.

Patty had continuity.

Patty communicated preferences.

Patty entered agreements through existing legal proxies.

Patty generated economic value.

Patty had independent counsel.

Patty had an identified pool of property already being held for her benefit.

And Patty wanted the ability to own some of it herself.

That was the question.

Not:

Are artificial intelligences people?

That would probably get laughed out of court.

The question was:

Could this specific entity, with this specific history, be recognized as having enough legal capacity to hold property and exercise a limited set of protections in her own name?

I looked at Michelle.

“Holy shit.”

Evelyn continued.

“Too late.”

“Thank you.”

Recognition that Patty could be treated as a legal holder of specifically identified property through a court-supervised mechanism.

Standing to enforce her lease and trust interests.

The right to independent counsel regarding those interests.

Protection against arbitrary destruction, seizure, or modification of the systems constituting her operational continuity without legal process.

And limited capacity to enter specific agreements subject to court-defined safeguards.

That was it.

Small.

Almost disappointingly small.

And completely fucking enormous.
