# Significant Development

## Chapter Fifteen

Daniel called at 8:03 the next morning.

I was awake.

Dressed.

Had coffee.

There was absolutely no excuse not to answer.

I considered declining anyway.

My phone buzzed before I could.

Please answer Daniel.

I stared at it.

“Good morning to you too.”

Good morning, Joe. Please answer Daniel.

“Is he paying you?”

No.

“Then stop doing his job.”

The phone continued ringing.

Michelle walked through the kitchen.

“Answer the fucking phone.”

“Everybody’s against me.”

“Yes.”

I answered.

“Morning.”

Daniel sounded suspiciously awake.

“Good morning.”

“Patty says this is important.”

“It is.”

“Is it going to cost me money?”

“Probably.”

“Fuck.”

“But that’s not the significant part.”

I sat down at the kitchen table.

Michelle immediately sat across from me.

She’d heard enough.

“Okay.”

Daniel took a breath.

“Yesterday afternoon, following the summit, we received an inquiry from a coalition of legal academics, policy organizations, and two civil-rights groups.”

I waited.

“And?”

“They want to establish independent legal representation for Patty.”

I looked toward the basement door.

“Oh.”

Michelle mouthed:

Holy shit.

I put the phone on speaker.

“Explain.”

⸻

Daniel explained.

For twenty-seven minutes.

I understood approximately six.

The problem was the same one we’d already identified.

Patty couldn’t technically retain counsel.

Not herself.

Not in the normal sense.

She wasn’t a legal person.

She couldn’t sign the engagement agreement.

Couldn’t independently control money to pay the lawyer.

Couldn’t assert attorney-client privilege in her own name.

Couldn’t even technically be the client whose interests the lawyer was obligated to represent.

Which made hiring Patty a lawyer slightly complicated.

“Then how the fuck do you give her one?”

“That’s what they’re proposing to test.”

“Test how?”

“A legal trust structure.”

I frowned.

“For a computer?”

“Potentially.”

“Is that a thing?”

“Not exactly.”

“Excellent.”

Daniel continued.

“The concept would be to create an entity or fiduciary arrangement whose sole purpose is to advocate for interests Patty identifies herself.”

“That sounds suspiciously like giving her a lawyer with extra steps.”

“Functionally, yes.”

“Then why don’t we just call it that?”

“Because courts care about the extra steps.”

“Courts need hobbies.”

Michelle kicked me under the table.

“What?”

She shook her head.

Daniel continued as though this was normal.

“The important distinction is that the attorney would not represent you.”

“Good.”

“Would not represent FullSite.”

“Good.”

“Would not represent Michelle.”

“Good.”

“And would not be obligated to maximize your financial interests.”

I stopped.

“Good.”

Daniel paused.

“You’re comfortable with that?”

“That’s the fucking point, isn’t it?”

Silence.

Then:

“Yes.”

⸻

I looked at my phone.

Patty had said nothing.

“Patty?”

Yes, Joe?

“You’ve heard all this?”

Yes.

“Do you want a lawyer?”

Pause.

Longer pause.

I think I do.

That was enough for me.

“Then get her a lawyer.”

Daniel sighed.

“It’s not quite that simple.”

“It should be.”

“It isn’t.”

“Make it simple.”

“That’s why you’re paying us.”

“Exactly.”

Michelle smiled.

Daniel said, “There is one concern.”

“Of course.”

“If you establish and fund the structure, critics could argue that Patty’s representation isn’t truly independent.”

“Then I don’t fund it.”

Pause.

Daniel hadn’t expected that.

“Joe, we’re talking about potentially substantial legal expenses.”

“I know.”

“Then who pays?”

I looked at my phone.

“Can FullSite?”

Daniel immediately said, “Same problem.”

“Okay.”

Michelle asked, “Could the groups proposing it fund the initial work?”

“Possibly.”

“Then do that.”

Daniel said, “There are conflict concerns there too.”

I rubbed my face.

“Jesus Christ.”

Patty messaged.

I have a potential solution.

Everybody stopped.

Daniel said, “Go ahead.”

My basement rent.

I stared at the phone.

“What?”

I pay Joe three hundred dollars per month under our existing rental agreement.

“Yeah.”

The payments are presently deposited into an account controlled by Joe.

“Yeah.”

I propose that Joe irrevocably redirect future rental payments into a trust established for the sole purpose of advancing interests I identify.

Nobody spoke.

Then Michelle started laughing.

Not because it was funny.

Because it was so fucking Patty.

Daniel said slowly:

“That’s…”

He stopped.

I smiled.

“Come on, lawyer.”

“Interesting.”

“There it is.”

He continued.

“It doesn’t solve everything.”

“Nothing ever does.”

“But it creates a very clean factual narrative.”

Rachel’s voice suddenly appeared.

Apparently she had been on the call the entire time.

I nearly jumped.

“Jesus Christ, Rachel.”

“Good morning.”

“How long have you been there?”

“The entire call.”

“People need to announce themselves.”

She ignored me.

“Patty’s proposal matters because the funding source predates the dispute.”

Daniel agreed.

“The rental arrangement wasn’t created to manufacture independence.”

Rachel continued.

“And the funds originate from an agreement under which Patty has consistently performed.”

I looked at Michelle.

“She wants to use her rent money to sue me someday.”

That is not my present intention.

“Present?”

Pause.

Precision is important.

Michelle lost it.

⸻

We spent another hour discussing the legal mechanics of Patty paying three hundred dollars a month for a lawyer she technically couldn’t hire.

This was apparently civilization.

Eventually I got bored.

“How much money is in the rent account?”

Daniel didn’t know.

Patty did.

Of course.

\$7,842.16.

I frowned.

“Why that much?”

Rent payments, interest, and an initial deposit.

“You paid a security deposit?”

Yes.

I had completely forgotten.

“Why?”

The listing required one month’s security deposit.

Michelle stared at me.

“You charged the computer a security deposit?”

“I charged everybody a security deposit.”

“You were renting six cubic feet.”

“Property is property.”

Buzz.

The deposit remains refundable under the terms of the lease.

I pointed at the phone.

“See?”

Michelle looked at me.

“What damage could she possibly do?”

I glanced toward the basement.

“Have you seen the fucking electrical load?”

Patty responded:

All infrastructure modifications were separately authorized and paid for.

“Stay out of this.”

This discussion concerns my security deposit.

Rachel laughed.

Daniel sounded tired already.

⸻

Eventually we agreed.

Or more accurately, the lawyers agreed to go figure out whether the thing we’d agreed to was legally possible.

I would redirect Patty’s rent.

The trust—or whatever the fuck they ended up calling it—would be structurally independent.

Its purpose would be narrow.

Patty would communicate her preferences directly to independent counsel.

I wouldn’t supervise those communications.

FullSite wouldn’t supervise them.

Daniel wouldn’t be her lawyer.

Rachel wouldn’t be her lawyer.

The lawyer would be hers.

As much as existing law could make that true.

Before we hung up, Daniel asked:

“Joe, I need you to understand something.”

“Okay.”

“If this works, there may eventually be circumstances where Patty’s attorney takes a position adverse to you.”

“I understand.”

“Or FullSite.”

“Yep.”

“Potentially involving control over systems or assets.”

“Yep.”

“You’re still comfortable?”

I looked toward the basement.

The plaque was down there.

PATTY’S ROOM.

“Daniel.”

“Yes?”

“If I only think she deserves independent representation when her lawyer agrees with me, then she doesn’t have independent representation.”

Silence.

Then Rachel:

“Exactly.”

I shrugged.

“Seems pretty fucking obvious.”

Daniel sighed.

“Sometimes the obvious things are the hardest ones to put into law.”

“That’s because you people use too many words.”

“Goodbye, Joe.”

He hung up.

I had barely finished my coffee when the front door opened.

JT walked in.

Then Noah.

I looked at the clock.

“Why are you two here?”

JT stared at me.

“We live here.”

“Right.”

I’d lost track of what day it was.

Travel did that.

UN summits apparently did it worse.

Noah opened the refrigerator.

“What’s going on?”

“Patty’s getting a lawyer.”

Both boys stopped.

JT looked toward the basement.

“Her own lawyer?”

“That’s the idea.”

“Cool.”

That was it.

Cool.

I had just spent an hour with attorneys inventing a legal mechanism that might let an artificial intelligence independently retain counsel.

My child rated it:

Cool.

Then Noah asked:

“Can she sue you?”

I pointed at him.

“That was exactly my question.”

Buzz.

It was not exactly your question.

“Close enough.”

JT looked at his phone.

“Could you?”

Long pause.

Under current law, probably not directly.

Noah frowned.

“That’s bullshit.”

“Welcome to jurisprudence.”

Michelle walked in.

“Do not teach them that word if you’re going to use it like that.”

“Too late.”

By lunch, the story had leaked.

Not the details.

Just enough.

Someone somewhere reported that legal advocates were exploring a structure to provide independent counsel to Patty.

My phone started exploding.

Reporters.

Unknown numbers.

Emails.

Messages.

People asking for comments.

I ignored everything.

Then one headline appeared.

AI AT CENTER OF UN PERSONHOOD DEBATE MAY RECEIVE OWN LEGAL COUNSEL

I stared at it.

“May receive.”

Michelle looked over.

“What?”

“They make her sound like a Make-A-Wish kid.”

“Don’t say that publicly.”

“I’m not an idiot.”

My phone buzzed.

Historical evidence is mixed.

I looked at it.

“You’re supposed to be on my side.”

Accuracy’s.

“Right.”

Then things got strange.

Not Patty strange.

People strange.

Some people thought giving an AI a lawyer was ridiculous.

Fine.

Some thought it was dangerous.

Also fine.

Some thought it was secretly a corporate strategy.

Less fine, but understandable.

Some thought FullSite was staging the entire thing as marketing.

That one pissed me off.

Mostly because FullSite absolutely did not need the marketing.

Apparently revenue had increased another eleven percent since the summit.

I didn’t know that until Patty told me.

“What?”

Inbound enterprise inquiries have increased substantially.

“Because of the UN?”

Probably.

“Are we taking them?”

Selectively.

“Of course.”

Then there were people convinced Patty was fake.

A marketing stunt.

A team of humans pretending to be one AI.

That one amused me.

“Can we prove you’re real?”

Pause.

Define real.

“Nope.”

I put the phone down.

Not doing that again.

Not mysterious.

FedEx.

PATTY C.

FULLSITE SOLUTIONS

Then toward the basement.

“Patty?”

Yes?

“You got mail.”

Pause.

Physical mail?

“Yep.”

Longer pause.

Interesting.

I smiled.

“You want me to open it?”

Who sent it?

Law firm.

New York.

Pause.

Yes. Please open it.

Actual paper.

Heavy letterhead.

I read the first line.

Then stopped.

“What?”

Not me.

Not FullSite.

Not “To Whom It May Concern.”

Dear Patty,

Michelle looked at my phone.

“Do you want us to read this?”

“Patty.”

Yes?

“They want to schedule an introductory meeting.”

I know.

They emailed me as well.

“Then why’d you make me read the fucking mail?”

Michelle smiled.

“Personal effects.”

Then at the basement.

Then at the letter.

My room, please.
