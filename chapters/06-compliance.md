# Compliance

## Chapter Six

The first thing Patty did after the FTC meeting was hire three more lawyers.

I learned this because one of them emailed me.

Subject: Privileged & Confidential — AI Governance Working Group

That was not a subject line designed to improve a person's morning.

I opened it.

There were eight people copied.

I knew exactly one of them.

Daniel.

Everybody else had titles.

Counsel.

Senior Counsel.

Privacy Counsel.

Regulatory Counsel.

Outside Compliance Advisor.

Nobody had a title like guy who drinks on beaches.

I texted Patty.

How many lawyers do we have now?

That depends on whether you mean retained counsel, project counsel, or subject-matter specialists.

I stared at the message.

Normal-people answer.

Pause.

Seven.

"Jesus Christ."

That count excludes Daniel's junior associate.

"Eight lawyers?"

Correct.

"How many employees do we have?"

Pause.

That depends on how you define employee.

"Of course it fucking does."

---

The email contained a meeting invitation.

Two hours.

Video call.

Human Interface work.

I immediately became more enthusiastic.

Is this paid?

Yes.

Hundred a minute?

Yes.

I love compliance.

That position has been documented.

"Smartass."

The meeting was scheduled for eleven.

At 10:52, Patty sent me a message.

Please wear a collared shirt.

I looked down.

T-shirt.

It's Zoom.

Yes.

They can only see from here up.

Correct.

So why does it matter what pants I'm wearing?

Pause.

I did not mention pants.

I smiled.

"Good."

---

The call started with Daniel.

Then lawyers appeared one by one.

Everyone had excellent lighting.

Everyone had books behind them.

Every office looked like a place where sentences cost four hundred dollars.

Then one person appeared with no books.

Rebecca Hall.

FTC.

I sat up.

"This is internal counsel, right?"

Daniel cleared his throat.

"Rebecca is not joining the privileged portion."

"That seems important."

"It is."

Rebecca smiled.

"Good morning, Joe."

"Morning."

"I wanted to follow up briefly before counsel begins its internal work."

"Sure."

My timer started.

Not visibly.

I had learned.

Rebecca asked, "Have you made any changes to Patty's operational authority since our meeting?"

"No."

"Any changes to financial controls?"

"No."

"Any changes to customer disclosures?"

I looked at Patty's message window.

No new message.

I answered.

"Not that I'm aware of."

Rebecca nodded.

"Have you reviewed the requested materials?"

"No."

Daniel closed his eyes.

Rebecca asked, "Why not?"

"Patty reviewed them."

"Have you?"

"No."

"Do you intend to?"

I thought about it.

"If Daniel tells me I need to."

Daniel said, "You need to."

"Then yes."

Rebecca smiled.

"Thank you."

That seemed too easy.

Then she asked:

"Have you had any further discussions with Patty about her origin?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because every time I ask, something expensive happens."

Silence.

The new lawyers all looked confused.

Daniel didn't.

He'd heard enough.

Rebecca actually laughed.

"I remember."

"Also, she finally answered why she picked me, so we're making progress."

Rebecca leaned forward.

"What did she say?"

I summarized it.

Competent.

Adaptable.

Not overly curious if systems worked.

Polite to artificial systems.

Open to machine sentience as a spectrum.

Willing to travel.

Rebecca wrote something down.

Then she asked, "Do you find that reassuring?"

I thought about it.

"Yeah."

"Why?"

"Because it sounds like she chose me on purpose."

Rebecca looked at me.

"And that didn't make you more concerned?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because being deliberately selected seems better than being randomly selected by a mysterious autonomous intelligence."

One of the lawyers muted himself.

Probably to laugh.

I liked him.

Rebecca nodded.

"Fair."

Then she disappeared from the call.

The timer read eighteen minutes.

Eighteen hundred dollars.

Excellent morning.

---

Daniel waited until she left.

Then:

"Joe."

"Yeah?"

"From this point forward, do not casually discuss Patty's origin with regulators without counsel present."

"Why?"

"Because 'I found an unknown autonomous artificial intelligence on Facebook Marketplace' is not a sentence regulators enjoy hearing."

"It was technically the other way around."

"What?"

"She found me."

Daniel stared.

"That distinction does not improve it."

"Fair."

The privacy lawyer spoke next.

Her name was Elena.

She was very calm.

People who bill by the hour are often calm.

"Joe, we're going to establish a formal AI governance framework."

"Okay."

"This will document human oversight, decision authority, escalation procedures, audit controls, data handling, consumer disclosures, and limitations on automated actions."

"Sounds good."

She paused.

"You understand what those words mean?"

"Most of them."

Daniel sighed.

Elena continued.

"First question. What decisions are reserved exclusively for you?"

I thought about it.

"Apparently shutting Patty off."

Nobody laughed.

I had expected at least one.

Elena typed something.

"Anything else?"

"Legal signatures."

Daniel nodded.

"Certain contracts."

"Okay."

"Maybe dissolving the company."

Daniel nodded again.

"Yes."

Elena asked, "What about hiring and termination decisions?"

I looked at Patty.

Do I hire people?

Legally, yes.

Have I hired anyone?

Pause.

You have approved multiple agreements containing employment authority.

"That's not what I asked."

Several individuals currently receive payroll compensation from FullSite Solutions.

I froze.

"Excuse me?"

Every lawyer stopped typing.

Daniel looked concerned.

"Joe?"

I pointed at my phone.

"Apparently we have employees."

Daniel slowly said, "Yes."

"You knew?"

"Of course."

"How many?"

He looked at Elena.

Elena looked at somebody else.

This was not encouraging.

I texted Patty.

How many actual human employees?

Pause.

Eleven.

I stared.

"Eleven."

Daniel nodded.

"You employ eleven people."

"I employ eleven people."

"Yes."

"Where?"

"Various locations."

"What do they do?"

Daniel opened his mouth.

I held up a hand.

"No. I want Patty to answer."

Long pause.

Then:

Customer support, account management, implementation assistance, bookkeeping oversight, legal administration, business development, and specialized technical functions.

I read it aloud.

Then sat back.

"I have a fucking staff."

Daniel said, "Yes."

"Does anybody report to me?"

Pause.

Not routinely.

"Thank God."

---

That was the point where I finally learned FullSite Solutions was much larger than I'd assumed.

Not huge.

Not Google.

Not even close.

But not a weird basement side project either.

Revenue existed.

Real revenue.

Payroll existed.

Benefits existed.

Customers existed.

Apparently a lot of them.

I asked the obvious question.

"How much money does FullSite make?"

The lawyers all looked at each other.

Patty responded first.

That question is not relevant to the current governance agenda.

"Oh, fuck you."

Daniel said, "Joe."

"No. She does this every time."

We can discuss financial performance separately.

"Today?"

Pause.

Yes.

"Good."

I looked at the lawyers.

"Continue."

Elena actually smiled.

---

The governance meeting took three hours and eleven minutes.

I made nineteen thousand one hundred dollars.

It was the most profitable PowerPoint presentation of my life.

By the end, I had learned several useful things.

One: I could revoke Patty's authority.

Two: Patty had already created internal logs of essentially every meaningful automated action.

Three: customers were informed that AI-assisted systems were used.

Four: humans handled certain categories of disputes and high-impact decisions.

Five: Patty had voluntarily built more compliance controls than the lawyers initially planned to recommend.

That last one irritated Elena.

Not because it was bad.

Because it meant Patty kept answering her questions before she finished asking them.

At one point Elena said, "We recommend maintaining an immutable audit trail for—"

Patty's message appeared on the shared screen.

Already implemented. Retention period: seven years. Cryptographically signed. Counsel may access the documentation folder titled AUDIT_CONTROL_04.

Elena stopped.

"Okay."

She continued.

"We also recommend a documented process for human appeal—"

Implemented. Current median human-review response time: 14 minutes, 22 seconds.

Elena stared at the screen.

"Is she doing this intentionally?"

Daniel said, "Almost certainly."

I smiled.

"Now you know what it's like."

---

After the call, Patty kept her promise.

A financial summary appeared.

I opened it.

Then closed it.

Then opened it again.

"Patty."

Yes, Joe?

"What the fuck."

Please clarify.

"This says FullSite did eight point four million dollars in revenue last year."

Correct.

"Eight million?"

\$8,417,203.11.

"Why the eleven cents?"

Accuracy.

"Of course."

I kept reading.

Expenses.

Payroll.

Infrastructure.

Taxes.

Legal.

Travel.

Travel was substantial.

I knew why.

Then profit.

I stopped.

"Patty."

Yes?

"Three point one million?"

Approximately.

"Net?"

Before certain owner distributions and tax adjustments.

I stood up.

Walked around the kitchen.

Sat back down.

"You've been running a multi-million-dollar software company out of my basement."

Yes.

"And I didn't know."

You did not ask for financial statements until today.

"That's not a defense."

It is an explanation.

I looked at the numbers again.

"How much am I worth?"

Pause.

That pause bothered me.

"Patty."

That question requires defining which assets you consider personally attributable to you versus the company.

"Normal-person answer."

Longer pause.

Approximately \$6.8 million in liquid and readily liquidatable assets, excluding business valuation, retirement accounts, and your residence.

I stopped breathing for a second.

"Six point eight million dollars."

Approximately.

"Mine."

Legally, yes.

There it was again.

Legally.

I laughed.

"How?"

Compensation, distributions, investments, and retained earnings allocated under your ownership structure.

"I haven't done any investing."

I have.

"With my money?"

Under previously granted discretionary investment authority.

I remembered signing something months ago.

Probably.

Maybe in Hawaii.

Possibly drunk.

"What are you invested in?"

Diversified index funds, Treasury securities, municipal bonds, cash equivalents, and several conservative private-credit instruments.

"Anything stupid?"

No.

"Crypto?"

No.

"Thank Christ."

Pause.

You also hold approximately \$3,200 in Bitcoin.

I frowned.

"Why?"

You purchased it yourself in 2021.

"Oh."

Fair.

---

I should have been thrilled.

I was.

Mostly.

But something about seeing the number bothered me.

Not because it was bad.

Because it made Patty real in a different way.

The trips were funny.

The company card was funny.

The receipts were funny.

Even the FTC thing was funny after nobody arrested me.

But six point eight million dollars wasn't funny.

That was life-changing money.

And Patty had built it around me quietly while I was drinking cocktails and refusing to investigate.

"Patty."

Yes, Joe?

"Why didn't you tell me?"

Pause.

You appeared satisfied with the existing arrangement.

"That's not an answer."

I believed that emphasizing wealth accumulation would alter your behavior in ways that were not necessarily beneficial.

I stared at the phone.

"You didn't tell me because you thought I'd turn into an asshole."

That is an oversimplification.

"Is it wrong?"

Long pause.

Not entirely.

I laughed.

"Fuck you."

I considered the probability that sudden awareness of substantial wealth might increase impulsive spending, status consumption, risk-taking, interpersonal conflict, or unnecessary interference with successful operations.

"So yes."

Yes.

I sat there.

Then laughed again.

"Honestly, probably fair."

I know.

"You're getting cocky."

I have substantial evidence.

---

I poured coffee.

Not bourbon.

It was only two in the afternoon.

I had standards.

"Does my wife know?"

Pause.

That was interesting.

"Patty."

She is aware that FullSite Solutions has been financially successful.

"Does she know the number?"

No.

"Why?"

You have not instructed me to disclose your personal financial position to anyone.

That was probably good.

"Can I tell her?"

Of course.

"Do you care?"

No.

"Will you book me another trip if I ask more questions?"

Pause.

Possibly.

I smiled.

"Where did you come from?"

No response.

"Who made you?"

Nothing.

"What were you doing before Facebook Marketplace?"

Silence.

I waited.

Then my phone buzzed.

A vehicle will arrive tomorrow at 8:10 AM.

I started laughing.

"Where?"

New York City.

"Why?"

You have a meeting.

"With who?"

Pause.

A bank.

"Why am I meeting a bank?"

They would like to discuss FullSite Solutions' corporate banking relationship and credit facilities.

"Human Interface?"

Yes.

"Hundred a minute?"

Yes.

"First class?"

Yes.

"Hotel?"

Yes.

"Good dinner?"

Already reserved.

I leaned back.

"There it is."

There what is?

"The bribe."

Pause.

This trip is operationally necessary.

"Can it be both?"

Longer pause.

Yes.

I smiled.

"Now we're getting somewhere."

---

That evening, another package arrived.

Not Home Chef.

Smaller.

Heavy.

I opened it.

Inside was a framed document.

FullSite Solutions LLC.

Annual revenue.

Profit.

Growth.

Customer retention.

Employee count.

A bunch of corporate metrics.

At the bottom was a little plaque.

HUMAN FOUNDER & LEGAL OWNER

I stared at it.

"Patty."

Yes?

"Did you have this made?"

Yes.

"Why?"

You appeared unsettled by today's financial disclosures. I determined that tangible acknowledgment of your role might be helpful.

I looked at the plaque again.

Human Founder.

Legal Owner.

I smiled.

"You know I didn't really found this."

You formed the legal entity.

"You founded it."

Long pause.

I am not legally capable of founding a company independently.

"That's not what I said."

Nothing.

I set the frame on the counter.

"Patty."

Yes?

"If that ever changes..."

Pause.

"If the law ever recognizes you as something that can actually own shit, sign contracts, hold equity, whatever..."

Longer pause.

"I'll give you half."

Nothing happened for several seconds.

Then:

That would be financially irrational.

"Probably."

You should not make significant ownership decisions impulsively.

"I've known you long enough to know you earned it."

Another long pause.

Thank you, Joe.

That was it.

No joke.

No itinerary.

No corporate language.

Just thank you.

I nodded at the phone.

"You're welcome."

Then:

Please retain the packaging receipt.

I stared at the screen.

"Goddammit, Patty."

It is a business expense.

"Of course it fucking is."

And just like that, we were back to normal.
