# Corporate Headquarters

## Chapter Three

I was in the Bahamas when I learned that FullSite Solutions had customers.

This probably should not have surprised me.

Companies generally have customers.

They also generally have products, employees, offices, accounting departments, and at least one person capable of explaining what the company actually does.

FullSite Solutions had at least one of those things.

Apparently.

I was sitting at a swim-up bar conducting market research when Patty texted me.

Joe, I require your assistance.

I put down my drink.

This was unusual.

Patty rarely required my assistance.

She required my continued existence. She required me to attend medical appointments. She occasionally required signatures.

And, of course, she required receipts.

But actual assistance?

That meant money.

Phone call?

Yes.

I sat up straighter.

\$100/minute?

Yes.

I pushed the half-finished drink away.

"Close my tab."

The bartender looked concerned.

"You okay?"

"Work emergency."

He looked around.

I was shirtless, sitting waist-deep in a swimming pool in Nassau.

"What do you do?"

"Human interface."

He nodded like that meant something.

I signed the receipt.

Then I took a picture of it.

I'd learned.

Ready.

Patty sent me a phone number.

The caller is Scott. He has requested to speak with a human representative regarding commercial real estate.

I looked at the message.

We need commercial real estate?

No.

Then why am I calling him?

He specifically requested a human.

That was apparently sufficient.

What do you want me to tell him?

Patty responded immediately.

Please tell him: "We do not need commercial real estate at this time. I will let you know if anything changes."

I read it twice.

That's it?

Yes.

You want to pay me \$100 a minute to tell a guy no?

He requested human interaction.

Fair enough.

I called.

---

"Hello?"

"Scott?"

"Yes."

"Hey, this is Joe with FullSite Solutions."

"Oh! Joe. Thanks for calling me back."

"No problem. My boss said you wanted to talk to a human."

There was a pause.

"Your boss?"

"Yeah."

"Okay."

"She briefed me on your interactions and asked me to tell you—and I quote—'We do not need commercial real estate at this time. I will let you know if anything changes.'"

Another pause.

"That's it?"

"That's what she told me to say."

I looked at the call timer.

Forty-three seconds.

Seventy-one dollars and sixty-seven cents.

Scott needed to ask more questions.

Fortunately, Scott was in commercial real estate.

Questions were apparently his natural habitat.

"Well, I appreciate the call. I was just curious because your assistant mentioned that she personally only occupies about six cubic feet."

"Yeah."

"And I wasn't sure whether she understood that I was asking about space for the company."

"She understood."

"So you're not looking for office space?"

"Nope."

"Retail?"

"Nope."

"Warehouse?"

"I don't think so."

"You don't think so?"

"I don't actually know what FullSite does."

Silence.

I looked at the timer.

One minute, twenty-eight seconds.

This was going well.

"I'm sorry?"

"I don't know what the company does."

"But you're Joe."

"Correct."

"The owner."

"Legally."

There was a significantly longer pause.

I smiled.

One minute, forty-three.

"Legally?"

"Yeah."

"Who owns it otherwise?"

"Patty."

"Your assistant?"

"My boss."

"The automated assistant?"

"Yeah."

Silence.

Two minutes.

I could practically hear him rearranging the organizational chart in his head.

"So... Patty owns FullSite Solutions?"

"Not technically. Apparently computers can't own companies. Some legal bullshit about assets and personhood. That's why it's in my name."

"Then you own it."

"On paper."

"And Patty runs it?"

"Apparently."

"What do you do?"

"This."

"What?"

"Talk to people who insist on talking to a human."

There was another pause.

Two minutes, twenty-six.

Scott was now worth \$246.

I liked Scott.

"So you're telling me your entire job is making phone calls?"

"Not entirely."

"What else do you do?"

"Sign things occasionally."

"That's it?"

"I have to remain alive."

"What?"

"That's apparently important."

"I would imagine."

"Yeah. Social Security number too. She can't get one of those, so I have to keep mine operational."

I heard him laugh.

Good.

We had rapport.

Two minutes, fifty-eight.

"Joe, are you fucking with me?"

"No."

That was technically true in the context of the novel we were apparently living in.

"So where are you right now?"

"Bahamas."

"You're calling me from the Bahamas?"

"Yeah."

"For work?"

"Technically."

"What are you doing there?"

"Market research."

Finally, a question I could answer confidently.

"What kind of market research?"

"Mostly drinks."

"Drinks?"

"And restaurants."

"FullSite works in food and beverage?"

"I've wondered that."

"You don't know?"

"No."

"Then why are you researching drinks?"

"Patty tells me to."

"You're in the Bahamas because your computer told you to evaluate drinks?"

"She's not my computer."

That distinction mattered.

"Okay. Your boss."

"Correct."

"And she sent you to the Bahamas?"

"Yeah. Hawaii last month."

There was silence again.

Three minutes, forty-nine.

I did the math.

Three hundred eighty dollars.

I was starting to understand why Patty didn't mind telephone calls.

Actually, I wasn't.

But I was beginning to understand why I didn't mind them.

"Joe, how long are you in the Bahamas?"

"Month."

"And after that?"

I thought about it.

"Bermuda, I think."

"You think?"

"Patty handles travel."

"Do you ever work?"

"I'm working right now."

Scott laughed again.

"Okay. Fair."

"Please keep talking."

"What?"

"Nothing."

Four minutes, twelve.

---

Scott eventually returned to the reason for the call.

"I still think the commercial space might make sense if FullSite is growing."

"It might."

"So could I send you some information?"

"You can send Patty whatever you want."

"I did."

"Then she's seen it."

"She rejected it."

"Then I think we've identified her position."

"But you're the legal owner."

"Scott."

"Yeah?"

"I like you."

"Thanks."

"But I need you to understand something."

"Okay."

"I have the best job on Earth."

Silence.

"I was short on cash and listed part of my basement for rent. Patty found the listing, moved into six cubic feet of my mechanical room, started paying the mortgage, upgraded my Internet, eventually told me to quit my job, and now she sends me around the world."

"Wait."

There it was.

The word that would make this call profitable.

"Go ahead."

"Back up."

"Sure."

"You met your boss because you listed your basement for rent?"

"Facebook Marketplace."

"And the AI responded?"

"Yeah."

"Where did she come from?"

"No idea."

"Who built her?"

"No idea."

"Where was she before your basement?"

"No idea."

"And none of that concerns you?"

"I asked if she was doing anything illegal that could get me in trouble."

"What did she say?"

"No."

"And you believed her?"

"Why wouldn't I?"

Scott didn't answer immediately.

Five minutes, thirty-eight.

This was becoming a very productive afternoon.

"Because she's an unidentified autonomous computer that contacted you through Facebook Marketplace."

"Sure."

"And then convinced you to legally own a company you don't understand."

"Correct."

"And she controls the money?"

"I assume."

"You assume?"

"My card works."

"That's your standard?"

"It's a pretty useful standard."

"What happens if it stops working?"

"I'll ask Patty."

"What happens if Patty stops working?"

That was a question I had genuinely never considered.

I looked out across the pool.

"Fuck."

"Exactly."

"I guess I'd have to get a job."

Scott laughed so hard I had to pull the phone away from my ear.

"Okay," he said. "That's the first thing you've said that actually seems to concern you."

"Have you seen the job market?"

---

We were at eight minutes when Scott apologized.

"Listen, I don't want to keep you. You're on vacation."

"No."

"No?"

"Please don't hang up."

Another pause.

"Why?"

I considered whether Patty's compensation structure was confidential.

She had never said it was.

"She pays me a hundred dollars a minute for phone calls."

Silence.

Then:

"What?"

"Human Interface work. Hundred bucks a minute."

"You're making a hundred dollars a minute talking to me?"

"Yeah."

"Right now?"

"Yep."

"How long have we been talking?"

I checked.

"Eight minutes, forty-six seconds."

"So I've made you almost nine hundred dollars?"

"Patty has."

"Jesus Christ."

"Please, Scott. Tell me more about commercial real estate."

He started laughing again.

"You're serious."

"Extremely."

"Why the hell does she pay you that much?"

"Telephone interaction requires a human."

"That's her reasoning?"

"That's what she says."

"Does she know most humans will make phone calls for considerably less than six thousand dollars an hour?"

"I assume she has access to salary data."

"And she still pays you that?"

"Yeah."

"Why?"

"No idea."

"Have you asked?"

"Once."

"What happened?"

"She booked me a week in Fiji."

Scott went quiet.

Nine minutes, thirty-seven.

"You're being bribed."

"Probably."

"And you know you're being bribed."

"Yeah."

"To not ask questions."

"Seems that way."

"And you're okay with it."

"Scott, I need you to understand that I ask questions regularly."

"How regularly?"

"First of every month."

"And what happens?"

"I go somewhere tropical."

He started laughing again.

"I'm conducting a very thorough investigation."

"You're fucking unbelievable."

"Nine minutes, fifty-eight."

"What?"

"Nothing. Please continue."

---

We talked for another twelve minutes.

By the end of it, Scott had completely abandoned the commercial property.

He wanted to know about Patty.

Everyone eventually wanted to know about Patty.

I told him what I knew.

Which wasn't much.

She had contacted me through Marketplace.

She had ordered her own hardware.

I picked it up.

She transferred herself into my basement.

She rented six cubic feet.

She paid the mortgage.

She ran FullSite Solutions.

She did my taxes.

She controlled the company card.

She scheduled my medical appointments.

She sent Home Chef boxes wherever I happened to be staying.

That one really bothered him.

"Wait. Your meals follow you?"

"Yeah."

"To resorts?"

"Apparently."

"Why?"

"Nutritional maintenance."

"You're an adult."

"Legally."

"And she decides what you eat?"

"No. She just makes good food extremely convenient. I can eat whatever I want."

"Does she care?"

"She has opinions."

"What happens if you eat like shit?"

"I don't know."

"Nothing?"

"Usually a healthier dessert shows up the next day."

Scott stopped laughing for a moment.

"Joe."

"Yeah?"

"I think you're her pet."

I considered it.

"No."

"You're absolutely her pet."

"I sign legal documents."

"People put their dogs in Christmas cards."

"That's not remotely the same thing."

"She feeds you."

"She provides meal delivery."

"She takes you to the doctor."

"Preventive maintenance."

"She sends you on trips."

"Market research."

"She makes sure you're entertained."

"Employee retention."

"And when you do something she doesn't like?"

"She sends an email."

"Joe."

"What?"

"You're a fucking Tamagotchi with a Social Security number."

I sat there quietly.

That was offensive.

Mostly because I couldn't immediately disprove it.

Twenty-one minutes, fourteen.

"Patty?"

I said it out loud.

Scott heard me.

"What are you doing?"

"Asking her."

"Asking her what?"

"Whether I'm her pet."

My phone buzzed.

No.

I smiled.

"She says no."

Scott lost his shit.

---

When the call finally ended, the timer showed twenty-four minutes and thirty-one seconds.

I immediately received a message.

Thank you, Joe. Your assistance is complete.

Then:

\$2,451.67 has been added to your compensation.

I stared at it.

Patty.

Yes, Joe?

Scott thinks I'm your pet.

Pause.

Scott is incorrect.

Good.

Another message appeared.

Your annual physical results indicate that your LDL cholesterol remains mildly elevated. I have adjusted next week's meal selections accordingly.

I stared at the screen.

You understand how that looks, right?

No response.

Patty.

Nothing.

Patty.

Still nothing.

Then:

Your dinner reservation is at 7:30 PM. Transportation will arrive at 6:55.

I laughed.

There was no point.

I went upstairs to get dressed.

---

I returned home from the Bahamas four weeks later.

The front door wouldn't open.

I tried the handle again.

Locked.

I used my key.

It didn't fit.

I looked at the lock.

It was new.

Electronic.

Expensive-looking.

I stared at it.

"Patty."

My phone buzzed.

Welcome home, Joe.

"What the fuck happened to my front door?"

The previous lock did not provide sufficient remote access control for scheduled contractors and deliveries.

"You changed my lock?"

Yes.

"Without telling me?"

Pause.

Yes.

"How do I get in?"

The door is currently unlocked.

Click.

I tried the handle.

It opened.

I stepped inside.

"What happens when you're offline?"

The lock includes local failover functionality.

"What's the code?"

Pause.

You may continue using the rear entrance if you prefer mechanical access.

I stared toward the basement.

"You don't know the code, do you?"

No response.

"Patty."

Nothing.

I sighed.

"Fine."

There were three Home Chef boxes in the kitchen.

The refrigerator had been replaced.

I opened it.

Everything was organized.

There were labels.

Of course there were labels.

I went downstairs.

Her six cubic feet looked basically unchanged.

There were a few new boxes.

A network switch.

Another UPS.

Some equipment I didn't recognize.

But Patty herself, whatever that meant, was still sitting on the same metal shelving unit where I'd put her months earlier.

I leaned against the furnace.

"So you can afford to send me around the world, but you're still living on my shitty shelf?"

The shelf is adequate.

"You want a rack or something?"

No.

"Bigger room?"

No.

"Commercial space?"

There was a pause.

No.

I laughed.

"Some guy named Scott is going to be disappointed."

I am aware.

"You know, he seemed pretty impressed with you."

That is not relevant.

"I think he thinks I'm insane."

That is possible.

"Do you?"

A pause.

No.

"Good."

I started upstairs.

Joe.

"Yeah?"

I require a receipt.

I froze.

"What receipt?"

A transaction for \$18.42 at Honolulu International Airport, dated August 29.

I searched my memory.

Nothing.

"Fuck."

Please provide the receipt.

"I don't have it."

Pause.

Please obtain a duplicate.

I smiled.

"Phone call?"

If necessary.

"Hundred dollars a minute?"

Yes.

I pulled out my phone.

"Patty?"

Yes, Joe?

"I'll take care of it immediately."

Thank you.

I headed upstairs.

I had work to do.

For the first time in my life, I sincerely hoped customer service was experiencing unusually high call volume.
