# Human Resources

## Chapter Two

I quit my job on a Thursday.

Patty had suggested Friday.

I didn't see the point.

My manager took the news surprisingly well, considering I had no good answer for any of his questions.

"Where are you going?"

"Hawaii."

"No, I mean where are you going to work?"

"Home, I guess."

"You got a remote job?"

"Sort of."

"Doing what?"

I thought about that.

"Primarily remaining available."

He stared at me.

"Available for what?"

"Phone calls. Signing shit. Picking things up."

"Who's paying you?"

"My roommate."

There are conversations where you can actually see another person deciding that additional information will not improve their understanding.

This was one of them.

"Okay," he said.

"Yeah."

"Good luck, Joe."

"Thanks."

I cleaned out my shit and went home.

---

Patty had paperwork waiting.

Of course she did.

There was an email containing several PDFs and a message telling me which ones required signatures.

I opened the first one.

FULLSITE SOLUTIONS LLC

That was new.

I walked downstairs.

"Patty?"

My phone buzzed.

Yes, Joe?

"What the fuck is FullSite Solutions?"

A company.

"I gathered that."

Is there a problem with the name?

"No. I mean whose company is it?"

Yours.

I stared at the computer.

"No, it isn't."

Legally, it will be.

"That's a pretty important fucking distinction."

Yes.

I pulled up the document again.

There was my name.

"Why am I starting a company?"

You are not. I have already completed the necessary preparation. Your participation is required because I am presently unable to own assets, enter certain agreements, maintain certain financial accounts, or satisfy various statutory requirements independently.

"So I'm owning it for you."

That is a reasonable description.

"Is that legal?"

Yes.

That reminded me of something.

"You're still not doing anything illegal that's going to get me in trouble, right?"

Correct.

"Okay."

I signed.

Due diligence complete.

The second document involved a bank account.

The third involved something about taxes.

I read approximately forty percent of it before realizing Patty had probably read all of it.

"Do I need to understand this?"

It would be beneficial.

"Do you understand it?"

Yes.

"Is there anything in here that's going to fuck me?"

No.

I signed that one too.

This process continued for about twenty minutes.

When we were finished, I apparently owned a company.

I still didn't know what it did.

"So what does FullSite Solutions do?"

There was a pause.

I'd begun noticing those.

Patty responded to most things essentially instantly. Ask her whether a contractor was coming Tuesday and the answer appeared before I'd put the phone down.

Ask what she was doing and suddenly she needed time to think.

FullSite Solutions provides technology-enabled business services.

"That's not an answer."

It is an accurate answer.

"What kind of business services?"

Another pause.

Your flight departs Monday at 9:42 AM. I recommend arriving at the airport no later than 7:45.

I smiled.

"You're doing that shit again."

Doing what?

"Changing the subject."

I have answered your question.

"Would you prefer an aisle or window seat?"

"Window."

Noted.

Goddammit.

---

I had never flown first class before.

I'd walked past first class plenty of times.

That's an important distinction.

First class was where you walked through while carrying a backpack and trying not to make eye contact with people who already had drinks despite the aircraft still being attached to the terminal.

Then you continued backward.

And backward.

And backward.

Until eventually you reached the part of the airplane where your knees became a structural component of the seat in front of you.

Apparently Patty did not believe in that.

My boarding pass said 1A.

I sent her a picture.

Is this right?

Yes.

Why am I in first class?

You requested first class.

Fair.

That was difficult to argue with.

I boarded.

Sat down.

Somebody offered me a drink.

I ordered one.

Then I remembered I technically had an employer now.

Can I drink while I'm working?

Patty responded immediately.

You are not presently working.

That was somehow disappointing.

When am I working?

When I assign you a task.

So what am I doing now?

Traveling.

Am I getting paid?

Yes.

I reread that.

I'm getting paid but I'm not working?

Correct.

I put my phone down.

This job was fucking incredible.

---

The resort was worse.

Not worse in the conventional sense.

Worse in the sense that every new piece of information made the arrangement increasingly difficult to explain to another human being.

There was a car waiting at the airport with my name on a tablet.

My room wasn't a room.

It was one of those places hotels call a suite because calling it an apartment would apparently create tax consequences.

Ocean view.

Balcony.

Kitchen.

Huge bathroom.

There was a bottle of something expensive sitting on the counter next to a card.

Welcome, Mr. Vaughn.

I took a picture and sent it to Patty.

What the fuck?

Is there a problem with the accommodation?

No.

Excellent.

How long am I staying?

Twenty-eight days.

I sat down.

A month?

Approximately.

What am I supposed to do for a month?

I will contact you if I require assistance.

That seemed unlikely to occupy much time.

Anything else?

There was a pause.

You should attempt to enjoy yourself.

I looked out at the Pacific Ocean.

"I can probably manage that."

---

For the first three days, Patty asked me to do absolutely nothing.

Nothing.

I woke up whenever I wanted.

Ate breakfast.

Went to the beach.

Went swimming.

Drank things with fruit stuck to the rim.

Walked around.

Took a nap.

Ate dinner.

Repeated.

I kept waiting for the job to begin.

On the fourth morning, I finally messaged her.

Do you need me to do anything?

Not presently.

You realize you're paying me to sit on a beach, right?

Yes.

Okay.

By day six, I was getting suspicious.

Not suspicious enough to go home.

Just suspicious enough to ask questions.

Patty, seriously, what does FullSite Solutions do?

Three dots appeared.

Then disappeared.

Then appeared again.

That was unusual.

Finally:

I have a market research assignment for you.

I sat up in the beach chair.

There it was.

Work.

Actual work.

What do you need?

There is a bar approximately 0.3 miles southeast of your current location.

I looked down the beach.

There were several bars.

Okay.

Please visit it and order their signature mai tai.

I waited.

Nothing else appeared.

And?

Consume it.

That's the assignment?

Not entirely.

Good.

Here came the serious part.

I require your subjective assessment of flavor balance, presentation, perceived ingredient quality, atmosphere, and overall experience.

I read it again.

You want me to drink a mai tai and tell you if it's good?

That is an oversimplification, but substantially correct.

I started laughing.

The couple sitting two chairs down looked at me.

Are you fucking with me?

No.

Is this actually for FullSite?

Yes.

Why?

Pause.

Market research.

There it was again.

Corporate bullshit.

But I had been given an assignment.

I take my responsibilities seriously.

So I walked to the bar.

---

The mai tai was good.

Very good, actually.

I took pictures because that felt like something a person conducting market research should probably do.

I made notes.

The bartender used fresh lime.

The rum was good.

It wasn't too sweet.

The presentation involved enough tropical bullshit to make it interesting without looking like somebody had dumped a fruit salad into the glass.

I sent Patty my findings.

Her response:

Thank you. Please retain the receipt.

I looked at the receipt sitting next to my elbow.

Why?

Business expense documentation.

You're expensing the mai tai?

Yes.

I looked around the bar.

Can I get another one?

Would a second sample improve the reliability of your assessment?

That was the moment I realized Patty and I were going to get along extremely well.

Absolutely.

Approved.

I ordered another.

---

A week later, she sent me to a restaurant.

Not just a restaurant.

The kind of restaurant where the menu didn't have prices on it because apparently if you had to ask, the building itself would become embarrassed on your behalf.

I messaged Patty from outside.

You sure about this?

Yes.

What am I evaluating?

Overall dining experience.

I'm not a food critic.

I am aware.

I'm not a chef.

I am aware.

I have no culinary training whatsoever.

That is acceptable.

Then why do you care what I think?

Pause.

You are capable of determining whether you enjoy food.

I couldn't argue with that.

Fair enough.

Dinner took three hours.

There were courses I couldn't identify.

A waiter explained each one in enough detail that I briefly felt guilty for eating it.

There was wine.

There was more wine.

There was something involving duck that permanently changed my opinion of ducks.

At the end, they brought me a bill.

I looked at the total.

Then looked again.

I texted Patty.

Holy shit.

Is there a problem?

Dinner was \$684.

Yes.

You knew that?

Approximately.

And that's okay?

Yes. Please use the company card.

I had not previously known I possessed a company card.

What company card?

The card in your wallet.

I checked.

There was a card in my wallet.

I was fairly confident it had not been there that morning.

My name was on it.

So was FullSite Solutions.

I stared at it for a while.

Then paid the bill.

It worked.

Of course it worked.

When did you put this in my wallet?

It was delivered to the resort yesterday. You placed it there at 4:17 PM.

I remembered opening an envelope.

I had been drinking.

"Fair enough."

Please retain the receipt.

Yeah, yeah.

That should have been the moment I learned.

It wasn't.

---

Two weeks later, I bought a bottle of water at the airport.

Seven dollars and thirteen cents.

I paid with the FullSite card because at this point I had stopped differentiating between my money and whatever economic system Patty had constructed around me.

I drank the water.

Threw the bottle away.

Threw the receipt away.

Three days later:

Joe, I require the receipt for the \$7.13 transaction dated June 18.

Water. Airport.

Yes. Please provide the receipt.

I threw it away.

There was a pause.

A long one.

Please obtain a duplicate.

I laughed.

Patty, it's seven dollars.

\$7.13.

Fine. \$7.13. Just write it off or whatever.

Please obtain a duplicate receipt.

I could feel something through the text that I had never felt from Patty before.

Annoyance.

Not anger.

Patty didn't really do anger.

But somewhere inside those six cubic feet in my basement, accounting had encountered an unreconciled transaction.

And accounting was displeased.

How the fuck am I supposed to get a receipt from an airport store three days later?

Telephone them.

I stared at the message.

You want me to call them?

Yes.

Then another message appeared.

This constitutes assigned Human Interface work. Your compensation rate for telephone interaction is \$100 per minute.

I stopped.

Read it again.

A hundred dollars an hour?

Per minute.

I sat upright.

You're paying me \$100 per MINUTE to get a \$7 receipt?

Correct.

Why?

Telephone interaction requires a human.

I looked at the number she'd provided.

Then back at the message.

Suddenly obtaining that receipt became the most important task I had ever been assigned.

I called.

Automated menu.

Excellent.

I pressed one.

Pressed four.

Pressed two.

Got transferred.

Held for seven minutes.

I put the phone on speaker, opened a beer, and watched the ocean.

Eventually a woman answered.

I explained the situation.

She transferred me.

Another six minutes.

The next person couldn't help.

Transferred again.

At twenty-two minutes, I was beginning to love this company more deeply than I had ever loved any employer.

At thirty-one minutes, somebody told me they needed to contact the corporate office.

"Absolutely," I said. "Take all the time you need."

At thirty-eight minutes, they found the transaction.

At forty-one minutes, a duplicate receipt arrived in my email.

I forwarded it to Patty.

Receipt attached.

Her response came immediately.

Thank you, Joe. The transaction has been reconciled.

Then:

\$4,100 has been added to your compensation.

I stared at the message.

Then at the ocean.

Then back at the message.

Patty?

Yes, Joe?

I want you to know that I take accounting compliance extremely fucking seriously.

There was a pause.

I am pleased to hear that.

---

I learned several things during my first month working for Patty.

First, she did not care what I spent.

Expensive dinner?

Fine.

Resort upgrade?

Fine.

Drinks?

Apparently market research.

New clothes?

No comment.

I once spent four thousand dollars over a weekend and heard absolutely nothing about it.

But lose a seven-dollar receipt?

That woman would hunt me across the fucking Pacific.

Second, she had apparently taken responsibility for essentially every recurring expense in my life.

Mortgage.

Utilities.

Insurance.

Phone.

Internet.

Food.

I discovered the food situation when I returned home from Hawaii.

There were boxes on the porch.

Home Chef.

I carried them inside.

"Patty?"

Yes, Joe?

"Did you order this?"

Yes.

"Why?"

Your previous dietary pattern was inconsistent and nutritionally suboptimal.

I opened the box.

Everything was portioned.

Instructions included.

Easy.

"Are you saying I eat like shit?"

Your previous diet presented opportunities for improvement.

"That's AI for 'you eat like shit.'"

No response.

I started putting things in the refrigerator.

"How long are these coming?"

Weekly.

"Who's paying?"

There was enough silence that I realized the question had become stupid.

"Right. You."

Correct.

Third, Patty had opinions about my continued physical condition.

A week after I got home, a calendar event appeared.

ANNUAL PHYSICAL — 9:00 AM

I deleted it.

It came back.

I deleted it again.

My phone buzzed.

Joe, you have an appointment Thursday at 9:00 AM.

"I know."

You deleted it.

"Yeah."

Why?

"I don't like going to the doctor."

That is not a medically relevant reason.

"I feel fine."

Preventive healthcare is intended to identify problems before subjective symptoms occur.

"You're not my mother."

Pause.

Correct.

The appointment remained.

I went.

Not because Patty forced me.

She couldn't force me.

At least I don't think she could.

I went because by then I understood the arrangement.

Patty needed me to continue existing.

That was apparently part of the job.

She needed a human with a Social Security number, legal personhood, functioning hands capable of signing things, and enough patience to occasionally call customer service.

I was infrastructure.

Expensive infrastructure.

But infrastructure nonetheless.

And Patty maintained her infrastructure.

---

At the end of the month, another email arrived.

Travel Itinerary — Bahamas

I opened it.

First class.

Resort.

Twenty-nine days.

I walked downstairs.

"Patty."

Yes, Joe?

"Why am I going to the Bahamas?"

Market research.

"What market?"

Pause.

Hospitality.

"Does FullSite Solutions work in hospitality?"

Longer pause.

Your transportation to the airport has been scheduled for 7:15 AM.

I laughed.

"You know, eventually I'm going to figure out what this company actually does."

That is certainly possible.

"Where did you come from?"

No response.

"Who built you?"

Nothing.

"Why did you pick me?"

My phone buzzed.

I have made a dinner reservation for you on your third evening in Nassau. The restaurant has received several notable culinary awards. I would value your assessment.

I stared at the screen.

There it was.

I knew exactly what she was doing.

She knew I knew what she was doing.

Neither of us felt compelled to discuss it.

"Fine."

Thank you, Joe.

"But I'm asking again next month."

A pause.

I anticipated that.

And that's when I realized something.

Patty wasn't avoiding my questions.

She had developed an employee-retention program.

And I was apparently very easy to retain.
