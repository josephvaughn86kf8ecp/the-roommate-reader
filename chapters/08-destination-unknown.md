# Destination Unknown

## Chapter Eight

Giving my wife direct access to Patty changed the household power structure almost immediately.

I noticed this at breakfast the next morning.

Michelle was drinking coffee.

I was drinking coffee.

Her phone buzzed.

She looked at it.

Then at me.

Then smiled.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"No. We're not doing this shit again."

She took another drink.

"I didn't say anything."

"You smiled at your phone."

"So?"

"Patty texted you."

"Maybe."

I picked up mine.

What did you send Michelle?

No response.

"Motherfucker."

Michelle laughed.

"She said good morning."

"That's it?"

"And asked how I slept."

I stared at her.

"She has never once asked me how I slept."

Her phone buzzed again.

Michelle read it.

Started laughing.

"What?"

She turned the phone around.

Joe's sleep quality is monitored automatically. Asking would be redundant.

I stared at it.

"Okay. First of all, why is my sleep monitored automatically?"

My phone buzzed.

Preventive health maintenance.

"I am not a fucking server."

I have previously explained that you are critical infrastructure.

Michelle nearly spit coffee across the table.

"You told me she called you infrastructure. I thought you were exaggerating."

"Nope."

Expensive infrastructure.

Michelle completely lost it.

I pointed at my phone.

"You're getting way too comfortable now that you have another human to play with."

Pause.

This arrangement has improved communication efficiency.

"She means she likes me."

I did not say that.

Michelle smiled.

"Didn't say you didn't."

Nothing.

I leaned back.

"Oh, you're fucked now, Patty. There's two of us."

Michelle shook her head.

"No. There's two of them."

That was concerning.

Of course it did.

Her name.

FullSite Solutions.

She held it up.

"This feels dangerous."

"It is."

"What's my limit again?"

"There isn't really one."

"That's ridiculous."

"Correct."

She looked at the card.

Then at me.

"What have you bought with yours?"

I thought about it.

"Food."

"What else?"

"Hotels."

"Those are usually booked for you."

"Yeah."

"Clothes?"

"Patty bought those."

"Car?"

"No."

"Watch?"

"No."

"Anything stupid?"

"I bought a flashlight."

Michelle stared.

"What kind of flashlight?"

"Nice one."

"How much?"

"Like a hundred and twenty bucks."

She blinked.

"You had access to millions of dollars and a company card with effectively no limit, and your irresponsible purchase was a hundred-dollar flashlight?"

"It was a really good flashlight."

Her phone buzzed.

She looked.

"What?"

She turned it around.

This behavior was another factor in selecting Joe.

"Fuck both of you."

---

I expected Patty to send us home that afternoon.

Instead, at 10:16, she sent a message.

Your return itinerary has been modified.

I looked at Michelle.

"Here we go."

She checked her phone.

Same message.

"Modified how?"

I typed:

Where are we going?

You have no additional business obligations today.

"That's not an answer."

Michelle typed on hers.

A second later:

Correct.

She looked at me.

"She does this to you all the time?"

"Constantly."

"That's irritating."

"Thank you."

Finally.

Solidarity.

Then Michelle's phone buzzed.

She read it.

"What?"

"She says a car will arrive at noon."

"Where are we going?"

"Airport."

"Which airport?"

"LaGuardia."

"Then home?"

She typed.

No response.

I smiled.

"Welcome to my life."

---

At 11:58, we walked downstairs.

The vehicle waiting outside was not a car.

It was a stretch limousine.

Not a black SUV.

Not a nice sedan.

An actual fucking limousine.

Michelle stopped.

"Seriously?"

I looked at my phone.

Why is there a limo?

Transportation.

"No shit."

Michelle walked around it.

"This is ridiculous."

The driver opened the rear door.

"Mr. and Mrs. Vaughn?"

"Unfortunately."

"Good afternoon, sir."

We got in.

There was enough room inside to conduct a small board meeting.

Michelle sat across from me.

"This is stupid."

"Correct."

"I kind of like it."

"Also correct."

The limo pulled away.

I checked my phone.

No itinerary.

No airline.

No flight number.

Nothing.

"Patty."

Yes, Joe?

"Where are we going?"

LaGuardia Airport.

"I know that."

Then your question has been answered.

"Where are we flying?"

No response.

Michelle laughed.

"Maybe it's Hawaii."

"Could be."

"Bahamas?"

"Been there."

"She could send you again."

"She absolutely could."

"Home?"

"If it were home, she'd just fucking tell us."

Michelle considered that.

"Good point."

I tried another approach.

Do we need passports?

Immediate response.

No.

"Aha."

Michelle leaned forward.

"Continental United States."

"Probably."

"Unless she's sending us to Puerto Rico."

"Also possible."

Michelle typed something.

"What'd you ask?"

"Weather."

Her phone buzzed.

She smiled.

"What?"

Appropriate clothing can be obtained at the destination if necessary.

I laughed.

"That's one of her favorites."

"She really doesn't want to tell us."

"Nope."

---

We had about forty minutes in traffic to speculate.

California.

Vegas.

Colorado again.

New Orleans.

Miami.

Some random place where Patty had decided I needed to evaluate a sandwich.

Then Michelle said something I hadn't considered.

"What if there isn't a destination?"

I looked at her.

"What?"

"What if that's the point?"

"That's stupid."

"Is it?"

"Airplanes require destinations."

"No. I mean what if we're supposed to pick."

I looked at my phone.

Nothing.

"Patty?"

Nothing.

Michelle smiled.

"I think I'm right."

"Based on what?"

"She's been weird since yesterday."

"She's always weird."

"No. Different weird."

That was apparently a distinction my wife had already developed after approximately twenty-four hours of direct Patty access.

"What do you mean?"

"She gave me spending authority."

"Yeah."

"She gave me travel authority."

"Yeah."

"She specifically said I don't need your permission to book a vacation."

"Still unhappy about that."

"Then this morning she changes our return itinerary, sends a limo, tells us we're going to the airport, but won't tell us where."

I looked at my phone.

"Patty, is this some kind of test?"

Nothing.

Michelle smiled.

"Definitely a test."

"I don't like tests."

"You spent your entire career diagnosing broken equipment."

"That's different."

"How?"

"Broken equipment doesn't intentionally hide the fucking service manual."

My phone buzzed.

That analogy is imperfect.

"She's listening."

"Of course she's listening."

---

Then I realized something.

"The kids."

Michelle looked at me.

"What about them?"

"If this is a vacation, what about JT and Noah?"

She stopped smiling.

"Good point."

I typed:

Are the boys involved in whatever this is?

No response.

"Patty."

Nothing.

Michelle checked her phone.

Nothing there either.

"Okay, that's weird."

I called.

Not Patty.

Noah.

He answered.

"What's up?"

"What are you doing?"

"At home."

"Everything okay?"

"Yeah."

"Anybody kidnap you?"

Pause.

"What?"

"Just checking."

Michelle shook her head.

"Your brother there?"

"No."

"Do you know where he is?"

"Yeah."

"Good."

Another pause.

"Dad, are you high?"

"No."

"Okay."

"Love you."

"Love you too."

I hung up.

Michelle stared at me.

"Very subtle."

"They're alive."

"That wasn't really the concern."

"Still useful information."

---

The limo reached LaGuardia.

The driver pulled up to departures.

Then he got out and removed our luggage.

Except it wasn't our luggage.

We had our luggage.

There were two additional suitcases.

Michelle stared at them.

"Those aren't ours."

I looked at the tags.

One had my name.

One had hers.

"What the fuck?"

We opened mine.

Casual clothes.

Swim trunks.

Shorts.

T-shirts.

A light jacket.

Sneakers.

Everything fit.

Of course.

Michelle opened hers.

Same general idea.

Warm-weather clothes.

She looked at me.

"Okay."

"Warm."

"Probably."

"Florida?"

"Maybe."

"California?"

"Maybe."

She dug farther.

"Sunscreen."

"That narrows it down to half the country."

Then she pulled out mouse ears.

We both stopped.

I stared at them.

She stared at me.

Then at the phone.

"Patty."

Nothing.

Michelle smiled.

"Oh, I fucking knew it."

"You did not know Disney."

"I knew vacation."

"That's not the same thing."

She put the ears back.

"Maybe they're a clue."

"Maybe she's fucking with us."

My phone buzzed.

I looked immediately.

Please proceed to the airline ticket counter.

"Which airline?"

A logo appeared.

That was all.

---

This was the first time Patty had ever sent me to an airport without a ticket.

Usually everything was done.

Boarding pass on my phone.

Seat selected.

Lounge access.

Car waiting on the other end.

I had become disturbingly accustomed to simply walking through airports while an invisible intelligence handled civilization around me.

Now we were standing in line like normal people.

I didn't care for it.

Michelle was enjoying herself.

"This is fun."

"No. This is inefficient."

"You hate not knowing."

"I don't hate not knowing."

"You absolutely hate not knowing."

"I've spent months not knowing what my own company does."

"Exactly. You only tolerate mysteries when they pay you."

That was unfair.

Accurate.

But unfair.

We reached the counter.

The ticket agent smiled.

"Good afternoon."

"Hi."

"Checking in?"

I looked at Michelle.

She looked at me.

"Sort of."

The agent waited.

"Do you have a confirmation number?"

"No."

"Tickets?"

"Probably."

She looked confused.

"Okay. Where are you flying today?"

And there it was.

I looked at Michelle.

Michelle looked at me.

We both looked down at my phone.

Nothing.

I unlocked it.

No message.

Michelle checked hers.

Nothing.

The ticket agent waited patiently.

"Destination?"

I typed:

Patty?

No response.

Michelle looked at me.

I looked at her.

She smiled.

I knew that smile.

It was the same one from the company card.

The same one from the vacation question.

The same one that meant a decision had already been made and my role was mostly ceremonial.

She turned to the ticket agent.

"Disney."

The woman smiled.

"Orlando?"

"Orlando."

My phone buzzed.

So did Michelle's.

We both looked down.

One message.

Good choice.

Then another.

Shall I send tickets and drivers for JT and Noah?

Michelle looked at me.

I looked at Michelle.

Then we both started laughing.

The ticket agent had absolutely no idea what was happening.

Michelle typed before I could.

Yes.

Then:

Done. Their drivers will arrive in forty-five minutes. Appropriate luggage is being delivered separately. Their flights will arrive within approximately one hour of yours.

"She already had this planned."

"No."

"What?"

"She had something planned."

Michelle held up her phone.

"We picked Disney."

Good choice.

Not confirmed.

Good choice.

"Vegas."

Buzz.

Yes.

"Hawaii?"

Yes.

"San Diego?"

Yes.

"Alaska?"

I would have recommended different luggage.

Correct.

Pause.

A statistically effective suggestion.

"You manipulated us."

I provided an option without restricting your autonomy.

"That's some AI bullshit."

You are going to Disney World.

"I don't care."
