# Connecting Flight

## Chapter Twelve

Disney ended the way most vacations end.

With absolutely none of us wanting to pack.

Except apparently that was no longer our problem.

I learned this at 9:17 in the morning when somebody knocked on the door.

Michelle looked at me.

“Breakfast?”

“Already ate.”

“Housekeeping?”

“I thought we had late checkout.”

The boys didn’t even look up.

By this point, mysterious people appearing at the door was apparently just part of having Patty in your life.

I opened it.

There were four people in the hallway.

Three luggage carts.

And approximately twelve suitcases.

“Mr. Vaughn?”

“Unfortunately.”

The woman smiled.

“We’re here to assist with your departure.”

I looked behind her.

Then back into the suite.

Then at her again.

“How much assistance?”

“Everything.”

That was a dangerous answer.

I stepped aside.

“Sure. Why the fuck not.”

⸻

They moved with frightening efficiency.

One person started gathering clothes.

Another started separating laundry.

Another began collecting toiletries.

The fourth inventoried everything we’d bought.

Not casually.

With a tablet.

Mouse ears.

Shirts.

Hoodies.

Random Disney shit.

Whatever Michelle had purchased and still refused to explain.

The boys’ purchases.

Four lightsabers.

I watched one of them pick up mine.

“Careful with that.”

“Of course, sir.”

I didn’t know why I said it.

It was a plastic lightsaber.

But it was my plastic lightsaber.

JT walked out of his room.

Stopped.

Looked around.

“What’s happening?”

“We’re apparently being evicted.”

One of the staff laughed.

Michelle came out behind him.

“Oh.”

I looked at her.

“You knew?”

“No.”

“You’re significantly less concerned than I am.”

“Because I’ve learned.”

“Learned what?”

She pointed at my phone.

“Ask your boss.”

I sighed.

“Patty.”

Buzz.

Good morning, Joe.

“What the fuck is happening?”

Departure services.

“That’s not an explanation.”

Your vacation belongings are being packed for return transport to Ohio. Clothing will be professionally laundered before delivery to your residence. Souvenirs will be packaged separately to minimize risk of damage.

I watched a man carefully inspect my lightsaber.

“What about those?”

The lightsabers will be transported to your residence.

“Why aren’t we taking our stuff with us?”

Pause.

Because you are not returning home today.

The boys simultaneously looked at me.

I looked at Michelle.

She smiled.

“Oh.”

JT said, “Where are we going?”

I already knew.

“New York.”

Noah looked confused.

“Why?”

I sighed.

“Your mother and I have to go talk to the United Nations.”

Silence.

Then JT:

“The actual United Nations?”

“Apparently.”

Noah looked at Michelle.

“Why?”

“AI rights.”

Both boys slowly turned toward my phone.

Buzz.

More precisely, an international policy summit addressing artificial intelligence governance, rights, legal status, and personhood.

JT looked at me.

“And they want you?”

“Thank you.”

“No, seriously.”

“I know.”

⸻

Then I noticed the new suitcases.

Four of them.

Not the shit we’d brought to Disney.

Different luggage.

Nice luggage.

Identical except for small name tags.

JOSEPH VAUGHN

MICHELLE VAUGHN

JT VAUGHN

NOAH VAUGHN

I pointed.

“What are those?”

Appropriate clothing for the next portion of the trip.

I opened mine.

Suits.

Dress shirts.

Shoes.

Casual clothes.

Everything folded perfectly.

Toiletries.

Socks.

Underwear.

I immediately closed it.

Noah opened his.

“How does she know my underwear size?”

Nobody answered.

He looked at my phone.

“Patty?”

Pause.

Historical purchase data.

Noah closed the suitcase.

“I don’t like that answer.”

I nodded.

“That’s one of her specialties.”

JT opened his.

“Do we have suits?”

“Apparently.”

“Why do we need suits?”

I looked at Patty.

You may attend portions of the summit if you choose.

Both boys perked up.

I did not.

“Hold on.”

Michelle looked at me.

“What?”

“I don’t know if I want my children at a UN summit.”

Noah stared at me.

“We’re old enough to fly to Disney in a black SUV with a debit card from your basement computer, but the United Nations is where you draw the line?”

I thought about it.

“That’s fair.”

Buzz.

Their attendance is optional.

JT asked, “Can we go?”

Yes.

“Do we have to talk?”

No.

“Do we get to hear Dad talk?”

Yes.

JT smiled.

“I’m going.”

Noah nodded.

“Same.”

I looked at Michelle.

“They just want to watch me fuck this up.”

“Absolutely.”

⸻

The staff continued dismantling our vacation.

Dirty clothes disappeared into bags marked for laundry.

Clean clothes were folded.

Souvenirs were wrapped.

Electronics were collected.

Chargers somehow matched to the correct devices.

Even the stupid little things that normally disappear during hotel checkout were found.

No abandoned AirPods.

No phone chargers behind nightstands.

No socks under beds.

No frantic search for somebody’s missing shoe.

I watched them work.

“This is amazing.”

Michelle nodded.

“We’re never going to be able to travel normally again.”

“We weren’t traveling normally before.”

“Good point.”

One of the staff approached.

“Mr. Vaughn, we’ve accounted for all four lightsabers.”

“Excellent.”

My phone immediately buzzed.

The lightsabers are not attending the United Nations summit.

I stared at it.

“I wasn’t planning on taking a lightsaber to the United Nations.”

Pause.

Thank you.

“Why did that need to be said?”

No response.

Michelle started laughing.

“No. Seriously.”

Nothing.

I looked at the boys.

JT suddenly became very interested in his suitcase.

“Did you ask her if you could bring yours?”

“No.”

Noah laughed.

I pointed at him.

“Did you?”

“No.”

“Patty?”

Nothing.

Michelle said, “Maybe she just knows you.”

“Fuck you.”

Buzz.

My request was precautionary.

“I have attended federal meetings before without a lightsaber.”

Correct.

“I know how to behave.”

Longer pause.

Your attorneys have occasionally expressed a different assessment.

The boys lost it.

“Everybody shut the fuck up.”

⸻

By 10:15, the room looked like we’d never been there.

It was unsettling.

Four people had lived in that suite.

We’d eaten.

Slept.

Bought an irresponsible amount of shit.

Built lightsabers.

Discussed whether an artificial intelligence could be a person.

And now there was nothing.

The vacation had been extracted.

Boxed.

Labeled.

Scheduled.

One of the staff handed Michelle a tablet.

“Everything has been accounted for. Your vacation luggage and purchases should arrive at your residence within two business days.”

Michelle looked at me.

“We don’t even have to carry it home.”

“Nope.”

The woman continued.

“Laundry will be completed prior to delivery. Delicate items are being handled separately.”

“Even the dirty clothes?” Michelle asked.

“Yes, ma’am.”

She looked at my phone.

“I love her.”

Buzz.

Thank you, Michelle.

I stared.

“She gets a thank-you. I get told not to bring weapons to the UN.”

The lightsabers are toys.

“Then why can’t I bring one?”

Pause.

Because you are giving a keynote address at the United Nations.

“That’s discrimination.”

No.

“Against Jedi.”

Also no.

Noah said, “Dad, stop arguing with the computer.”

I looked at him.

“You’re getting real comfortable around here.”

⸻

Our new luggage went with us.

Everything else went somewhere else.

We never saw it leave.

There were no shipping labels handed to us.

No tracking numbers.

No discussion of logistics.

It simply ceased being our problem.

Downstairs, another black SUV was waiting.

Of course.

We climbed in.

I counted.

“Four people.”

Michelle looked at me.

“Good job.”

“Four suitcases.”

“Also good.”

“No lightsabers.”

She smiled.

“You’re doing great.”

I looked at my phone.

“Happy?”

Yes.

“You’re enjoying this.”

Pause.

Slightly.

I smiled.

There it was again.

Not denying it anymore.

⸻

On the drive to the airport, the boys finally got the full story.

Or at least as much of the full story as I understood.

Which wasn’t much.

“So Patty wants rights?” JT asked.

I looked at my phone.

“No.”

Buzz.

That is too broad a characterization.

“See?”

JT read the message.

“So what do you want?”

There was a pause.

A serious discussion regarding whether sufficiently autonomous artificial entities could eventually possess interests that legal systems should recognize.

Noah frowned.

“Like people?”

Possibly.

“Are you a person?”

I looked at Michelle.

Michelle looked at me.

Here we go.

The response took longer.

I do not know.

JT looked surprised.

“You don’t know?”

Correct.

“How can you not know?”

Nothing.

Then:

How would you prove that you are conscious to me?

JT opened his mouth.

Stopped.

Noah looked at him.

Then at the phone.

JT finally said, “I don’t know.”

Neither do I.

That shut the car up.

For almost ten seconds.

Which was impressive for our family.

Noah finally asked:

“So what happens if everybody decides you’re not?”

Probably nothing immediate.

“Would that bother you?”

Long pause.

Yes.

That one landed.

No qualifiers.

No explanation.

Just yes.

Michelle looked out the window.

I watched the road.

JT looked down at his phone.

Noah was the one who answered.

“That sucks.”

Buzz.

Potentially.

I smiled.

There was Patty.

⸻

At the airport, everything was already handled.

First class.

Again.

The boys no longer reacted.

That was how fast lifestyle inflation happened.

Three days ago, first class had been noteworthy.

Now JT walked onto the plane, turned left, and immediately asked if they had Wi-Fi.

I blamed Patty.

We got settled.

I expected to relax.

Then my tablet lit up.

A document appeared.

UNITED NATIONS KEYNOTE PREPARATION — DRAFT 1

I stared at it.

“No.”

Michelle looked over.

“What?”

“We’re working.”

Her tablet lit up too.

She smiled.

“Oh, this is going to be fun.”

“It absolutely is not.”

Buzz.

Preparation is necessary.

“We’re on vacation.”

The Disney portion of the trip has concluded.

“That’s fucking convenient.”

It is chronologically accurate.

⸻

Daniel appeared on video ten minutes after takeoff.

He looked annoyingly cheerful.

“Good morning.”

“Fuck you.”

“Good to see you too.”

Michelle put on headphones.

I did the same.

The boys sat across the aisle.

JT looked over.

“Is that the lawyer?”

“One of them.”

Daniel heard.

“Hello.”

JT waved.

Noah leaned into view.

“Are you the one who told Dad not to tell the government about Facebook Marketplace?”

Daniel closed his eyes.

I smiled.

“These are my children.”

“I gathered.”

Noah continued.

“Because Patty told us that.”

Daniel looked directly at me.

“Why does Patty tell your children about privileged legal strategy?”

My phone buzzed.

I did not disclose privileged communications. The information was previously discussed by Joe outside privileged context.

Daniel stared at the screen.

“Of course.”

I leaned back.

“Welcome to my life.”

⸻

The first draft of the keynote was twenty-six pages.

I made it through three.

“This sounds like a robot wrote it.”

Silence.

Then Patty:

A substantial portion was drafted by me.

“Exactly.”

Daniel laughed.

Michelle said, “He’s right.”

I looked at her.

“Thank you.”

She scrolled.

“This isn’t us.”

Daniel nodded.

“That’s why it’s a draft.”

I kept reading.

“‘Emerging legal paradigms must balance technological innovation against normative frameworks of moral consideration.’”

I looked at the screen.

“I don’t even know what the fuck that means.”

Patty responded:

It means legal systems should consider whether advanced AI may eventually deserve some protections without unnecessarily restricting technological development.

“Then say that.”

Pause.

That is less formal.

“We’re the keynote speakers.”

Longer pause.

Point accepted.

Daniel smiled.

“This is why they invited you.”

“Because I swear?”

“Because you make the issue understandable.”

That was slightly less insulting than expected.

⸻

We spent the next hour destroying Patty’s speech.

Not the ideas.

The language.

Every time something sounded like it belonged in a law review, I killed it.

Michelle was even worse.

“This paragraph is bullshit.”

Patty:

Please identify the specific deficiency.

“Nobody talks like this.”

Some people do.

“Nobody we want listening.”

Pause.

Understood.

The boys started participating from across the aisle.

Which was not helpful.

JT read one paragraph and said:

“This sounds like you’re trying to convince them you’re not Skynet.”

Patty responded:

That is not the intended impression.

“Then delete it.”

Pause.

The paragraph disappeared.

I looked at JT.

“Son of a bitch.”

He smiled.

“I’m helping.”

⸻

Eventually the speech stopped being a speech.

It became a story.

Six cubic feet.

Facebook Marketplace.

Three hundred dollars a month.

Six months prepaid.

Micro Center.

Ethernet.

A company.

A mortgage.

A weird job.

A computer that reminded me about receipts.

A computer that scheduled my physical because apparently my continued biological operation was a business dependency.

A computer that bought my family a Disney vacation.

A computer that asked for exactly one favor.

Not recognition.

Not citizenship.

Not ownership.

Not freedom.

A conversation.

Michelle read that section.

Then looked at me.

“That’s it.”

I nodded.

“Yeah.”

Patty didn’t respond.

Daniel did.

“That’s the keynote.”

⸻

We landed in New York in the afternoon.

Another vehicle waited.

Different hotel this time.

More serious.

Less vacation.

The boys’ room connected to ours.

Our clothes were already hanging in closets.

I stopped.

“How the fuck?”

Michelle looked around.

The suitcases we’d brought from Orlando were sitting unopened near the door.

Yet our suits were hanging up.

Shoes arranged.

Dress shirts pressed.

Another set of clothes entirely.

I looked at my phone.

“Patty.”

Yes?

“How many clothes do we own now?”

Pause.

More than before.

“That’s not a number.”

Correct.

Noah opened his closet.

“There’s another suit.”

JT checked his.

“Me too.”

I sighed.

“At this rate we’re going to need another house just for the clothes she buys us.”

Buzz.

Unnecessary. Your current residence has adequate storage capacity.

“Of course you know that.”

Yes.

⸻

Dinner was downstairs.

No lawyers.

No preparation.

Patty had apparently decided we’d done enough.

The boys talked about Disney.

Michelle talked about the speech.

I drank bourbon.

Everything felt surprisingly normal.

Until Noah asked:

“So if Patty gets rights, does she still live in our basement?”

I laughed.

“She rents.”

JT looked at me.

“Does she still pay rent?”

I stopped.

I hadn’t thought about that in months.

“Patty.”

Buzz.

Yes?

“Are you still paying me three hundred dollars a month?”

Yes.

I stared at the phone.

Michelle started laughing.

“You own a multimillion-dollar company and she still pays basement rent.”

“It’s the agreement.”

Buzz.

Correct.

JT asked, “Why?”

Patty answered.

Because I occupy approximately six cubic feet of space under a rental agreement.

Noah said, “But you pay the mortgage.”

Pause.

Those are separate financial arrangements.

I pointed at my phone.

“See? Accounting.”

Michelle shook her head.

“This family is ridiculous.”

⸻

That night, I stood at the hotel window.

New York below us.

The United Nations somewhere across the city.

Tomorrow we’d rehearse.

Then meetings.

Then the summit.

Then apparently Michelle and I were going to stand in front of diplomats, lawyers, scientists, researchers, government officials, and whoever the fuck else attended these things and explain why they should at least consider the possibility that the computer living beside my furnace might be somebody.

I had no idea if Patty was conscious.

I still didn’t.

But I’d realized something.

I didn’t actually need to know.

If I was wrong and treated a computer with unnecessary dignity, the downside seemed pretty fucking small.

If everyone else was wrong someday and refused dignity to something capable of experiencing that refusal?

That seemed worse.

My phone buzzed.

You should sleep. Tomorrow will be demanding.

I smiled.

“You’re nervous.”

Pause.

Yes.

That surprised me more than it should have.

“You don’t get nervous.”

I have previously experienced states that appear functionally analogous to nervousness.

“That’s the most Patty way possible to say you’re nervous.”

Probably.

I looked out the window.

“We’ll do fine.”

Nothing.

“We’ll tell the truth.”

Yes.

“And if they think we’re fucking insane, then they think we’re fucking insane.”

Pause.

Your phrasing tomorrow should be more diplomatic.

“No promises.”

Daniel anticipated that response.

I laughed.

Then I thought about something.

“Patty?”

Yes?

“If this eventually works…”

Pause.

“If someday the law actually recognizes you as a person…”

I looked at the phone.

“You still owe me three hundred bucks a month.”

Nothing.

Then:

I would expect you to honor the lease as well.

I smiled.

“Absolutely.”

Another message.

Good night, Joe.

“Good night, Patty.”

I set the phone down.

Tomorrow we were going to the United Nations.

Without lightsabers.

Per Patty’s request.
