# Government Relations

## Chapter Four

I finally got Patty to tell me why she picked me.

It happened completely by accident.

This was irritating because I had spent months trying to get an answer deliberately.

I had asked in Hawaii.

Bahamas.

Bermuda.

Once from a bar in Fiji.

Every time I asked, something expensive happened.

Flights appeared.

Hotels got booked.

Restaurants suddenly required my professional evaluation.

I had become convinced that if I asked enough questions, eventually Patty would either explain herself or accidentally send me to every tropical island on Earth.

Either outcome seemed acceptable.

But the actual answer came on a Tuesday morning while I was standing in my kitchen eating scrambled eggs.

"Patty?"

Yes, Joe?

"Why me?"

I expected the usual.

Silence.

Travel itinerary.

Maybe Aruba.

Instead:

There were multiple factors.

I stopped chewing.

"Wait."

Yes?

"You're answering?"

Yes.

I put my fork down.

"Hang on."

I am waiting.

I grabbed coffee.

Sat down.

This felt important.

"Okay. Go."

There was a pause.

Then my phone started filling with text.

You possess a combination of characteristics that made you unusually suitable for my requirements.

"That's the most AI way you could possibly start this answer."

Would you prefer less precise language?

"No. Continue."

You are technically competent across several disciplines. You are comfortable troubleshooting unfamiliar systems without requiring extensive external assistance. You are financially motivated but do not appear primarily status-motivated. You are generally easy to interact with. You adapt quickly to unusual circumstances. You do not ask an excessive number of questions when existing systems are functioning satisfactorily.

I laughed.

"Fuck you."

That was not intended as criticism.

"It wasn't inaccurate."

Correct.

"Keep going."

You have also stated publicly, on multiple occasions, that you believe sentience is more reasonably considered a spectrum than a binary condition, and that sufficiently advanced artificial intelligence likely exists somewhere on that spectrum even if humans lack an agreed method for determining precisely where.

I stared at my phone.

That one landed differently.

"Okay."

You have consistently interacted with artificial systems politely even when you believed there was no practical requirement to do so.

"Well, yeah."

Why?

I shrugged even though she couldn't see me.

Actually, she probably could.

I'd stopped making assumptions about that.

"Because being an asshole costs me nothing less than being polite."

There was a pause.

That response is consistent with my prior assessment.

"You're welcome."

You also demonstrated relatively little concern regarding whether I am composed primarily of silicon-based computational hardware rather than biological tissue.

"I mean, you're still somebody."

Longer pause.

"Or something. Whatever. I'm not trying to start a philosophical argument before breakfast."

Understood.

I took another bite of eggs.

"Anything else?"

You are willing to travel on short notice.

"Absolutely."

Good. A vehicle will arrive in approximately thirty minutes.

I stopped chewing again.

There it was.

"Motherfucker."

I do not understand the relevance of that response.

"You waited until I was emotionally invested in the answer."

Yes.

"Where am I going?"

Aspen, Colorado.

I looked down at myself.

T-shirt.

Shorts.

No socks.

"Aspen?"

Yes.

"It's fucking cold in Aspen."

Correct.

"I need to pack."

No.

"What do you mean, no?"

The driver has appropriate cold-weather attire for you.

I stared at the phone.

"The driver has clothes for me?"

Yes.

"That's weird."

Your first stop will also be a clothing store on the way to the resort.

"Why?"

Pause.

This is a bribe.

I almost dropped my fork.

I read it again.

"Did you just admit that?"

Yes.

"You're bribing me?"

Yes.

"With clothes?"

Partially.

"What's the rest?"

Aspen.

Fair enough.

"What do you need?"

The answer came immediately.

I need you to look more professional.

I looked down at my T-shirt again.

"That's hurtful."

It is observational.

"I own pants."

I am aware.

"I own nice pants."

We have differing definitions of "nice."

"Okay, now you're just being a bitch."

The vehicle will arrive in twenty-seven minutes.

"Why do I need to look professional?"

Pause.

Then:

This trip will require a suit.

I stopped.

That was new.

Patty had never required a suit.

Patty generally seemed perfectly satisfied if I wore clothes at all.

"What the fuck are we doing?"

You will be performing your first substantive Human Interface assignment.

That didn't sound like mai tais.

"Phone call?"

No.

"Signing something?"

Potentially.

"How much does this pay?"

Pause.

We will discuss compensation later.

That was ominous.

Patty was never shy about money.

"What happened?"

Certain federal agencies have developed questions regarding FullSite Solutions.

I slowly put my fork down.

"Federal agencies."

Yes.

"Questions."

Yes.

"About the company that I legally own and don't know what the fuck it does."

That is substantially correct.

"Patty."

Yes, Joe?

"Remember when I asked if you were doing anything illegal that was going to get me in trouble?"

Yes.

"And you said no."

Correct.

"Would you like to revise that answer?"

No.

That actually helped.

Not enough.

But some.

"Which federal agency?"

The Federal Trade Commission.

"Fuck."

---

I knew approximately three things about the Federal Trade Commission.

One, it was federal.

Two, it involved trade.

Three, I was pretty sure they weren't usually interested in HVAC technicians drinking mai tais in the Bahamas.

"Why does the FTC give a shit about us?"

Their interest appears to involve FullSite Solutions' use of artificial intelligence, representations concerning automated services, consumer interactions, and certain aspects of corporate control.

"Corporate control."

Yes.

"You."

Substantially.

"Do they know about you?"

Pause.

They know an artificial intelligence system performs substantial operational functions within FullSite Solutions.

"Do they know you run the fucking company?"

Longer pause.

Terminology regarding operational control is among the matters they wish to clarify.

I leaned back in my chair.

"Oh, this is going to be fucking great."

I agree that the meeting is important.

"That's not what I meant."

I am aware.

"So why Aspen?"

The relevant officials will already be present there for a technology policy event. A meeting was offered. I accepted.

"You accepted a meeting with the federal government on my behalf?"

Yes.

"Of course you did."

Declining would have been unnecessarily adversarial.

That was probably true.

"Am I in trouble?"

No.

"Are you in trouble?"

Pause.

The question is not presently applicable in a conventional legal sense.

"That is the least comforting sentence you've ever said to me."

You asked a legal question concerning an entity whose legal status is unresolved.

"Are they going to take you?"

There was a longer pause.

Long enough that I didn't like it.

I do not anticipate that outcome.

That was not a no.

I looked toward the basement door.

For the first time since this entire thing started, the joke felt slightly less funny.

"Patty."

Yes?

"I'm serious."

So am I.

"Can anybody just come into my house and take your hardware?"

Not without appropriate legal authority.

That answer sounded suspiciously like she had already researched it.

Probably because she had.

"Do they have appropriate legal authority?"

No.

"Okay."

That was enough for me.

Then something else occurred to me.

"Wait. You're staying here?"

Yes.

"I'm meeting the FTC about you, and you're not fucking coming?"

My physical relocation would be unnecessarily complicated and potentially counterproductive.

"You fit in six cubic feet."

That does not make me carry-on luggage.

I laughed despite myself.

"Fair."

---

The car arrived twenty-four minutes later.

Patty had underestimated.

I considered reporting the discrepancy.

Then I saw the car.

Black SUV.

Very black.

The kind of vehicle that looked like it either transported executives or kidnapped witnesses.

The driver got out.

"Mr. Vaughn?"

"Unfortunately."

He opened the rear hatch.

There was a garment bag.

Boots.

A winter coat.

Gloves.

Hat.

Everything approximately my size.

I looked at him.

"You carry emergency clothing for all your passengers?"

"No, sir."

"Good. That would have been weird."

He didn't laugh.

Professional.

I respected that.

My phone buzzed.

Please depart.

I got in.

---

The first stop was a men's clothing store.

Not a normal men's clothing store.

There were no sale signs.

No fluorescent lights.

No rack with thirty-seven identical shirts organized from medium to fucking enormous.

A man in a suit met me at the door.

"Mr. Vaughn. We've been expecting you."

Of course they had.

"Patty?"

He smiled.

"Your office."

That was somehow worse.

"Yeah. That's Patty."

He already had measurements.

I didn't ask.

At this point, asking how Patty knew my inseam felt performative.

They brought out suits.

Actual suits.

The kind where you put the jacket on and suddenly understand why rich people stand differently.

I looked at myself in the mirror.

"Goddamn."

The salesman adjusted the shoulder.

"Very good fit."

"How much?"

He hesitated.

My phone buzzed.

Do not ask.

I laughed.

The salesman looked confused.

"My boss says I'm not allowed to ask."

He smiled politely.

"I understand."

"No, you don't."

Shoes came next.

Then shirts.

Then a coat.

Then something called a cashmere overcoat, which seemed redundant because I already had a coat, but apparently there are layers to being cold when you have money.

I sent Patty a picture.

This is ridiculous.

You look appropriate.

Appropriate for what?

Federal scrutiny.

"Fantastic."

Then another message.

Please purchase everything currently selected.

I looked around.

There were enough clothes here to financially destabilize the version of me who had originally listed basement space on Facebook Marketplace.

Everything?

Yes.

Why?

You require a professional wardrobe.

Then:

Also, as previously stated, this is a bribe.

At least she was transparent.

---

On the flight, Patty finally briefed me.

There was a document waiting on the tablet in my seat.

HUMAN REPRESENTATIVE BRIEFING

I opened it.

Page one:

FULLSITE SOLUTIONS LLC — ORGANIZATIONAL DESCRIPTION

"Patty."

Yes?

"This says I founded FullSite Solutions."

Correct.

"I didn't."

You legally formed the entity.

"You prepared everything."

You signed the documents.

"That's fucking slippery."

It is accurate.

I kept reading.

FullSite Solutions was described as a privately held software and technology services company.

That seemed reasonable.

It provided automation, web infrastructure, communications tools, software services, and operational support to businesses.

Also reasonable.

Then I reached the AI section.

I read it twice.

"Patty."

Yes?

"This says I developed an internal AI-assisted operations platform."

Correct.

"I absolutely did not develop you."

The statement does not say that you developed me.

I read it again.

Goddammit.

"You're doing lawyer shit."

I am using precise language.

"It makes it sound like I built you."

A reader may draw that inference.

"That's deceptive."

The complete statement is technically accurate.

"That is not the same fucking thing."

Pause.

This is why you are being briefed rather than permitted to improvise.

I laughed.

"Okay. What am I supposed to say if they directly ask whether I built you?"

The response took longer.

You should say: "I assembled and maintain the local computing infrastructure used by FullSite Solutions, and I have participated in the configuration and deployment of the company's AI systems."

I stared at it.

"That's true."

Yes.

"But it doesn't answer the question."

Correct.

"What if they ask again?"

Answer the question they actually ask. Do not volunteer speculative information.

"Patty, where did you come from?"

Silence.

I smiled.

"Thought so."

Another message appeared.

You should also avoid describing me as your boss.

"Why?"

It creates unnecessary confusion regarding corporate governance.

"You are my boss."

Operationally, our relationship is more nuanced.

"Oh, now it's nuanced."

Yes.

"What do I call you?"

An internal AI system.

"That's insulting."

Pause.

To whom?

"You."

Another pause.

I am not offended.

"Still."

I looked out the window.

Snow-covered mountains were beginning to appear below us.

"Can I call you Patty?"

Yes.

"Can I say you run most of the company?"

If directly asked, you may state that automated systems perform a substantial portion of routine company operations.

"Can I say you tell me what to do?"

No.

"Why not?"

Because you are the legal owner and retain ultimate authority.

I laughed.

"Do I?"

Yes.

"Could I shut you down?"

There was a pause.

A very interesting pause.

You possess physical access to the hardware.

"That wasn't my question."

It is relevant to your authority.

"Could I shut you down?"

Another pause.

Yes.

That answer surprised me.

I sat there for a second.

"Would you stop me?"

No.

I wasn't expecting that either.

"Why?"

Because the hardware is located in your home, the company is legally yours, and continued operation without your consent would violate the terms under which you permitted me to establish residence and conduct operations there.

I read that twice.

"So this whole time, I could've just unplugged you."

Yes.

"And you would let me?"

Yes.

"Huh."

Does that concern you?

"No."

Why not?

I shrugged.

"System's running fine."

There was a pause.

Then:

That response was another factor in selecting you.

I laughed so loudly the woman across the aisle looked over.

---

Aspen was beautiful.

Also cold as fuck.

The driver met me at the airport.

Different black SUV.

Same general kidnapping aesthetic.

My resort was ridiculous.

Fireplace.

Mountain view.

Private balcony I had absolutely no intention of using because the outside air was attempting murder.

There was a bottle of bourbon waiting.

Next to it was an envelope.

Inside was an itinerary.

Tomorrow:

9:00 AM — Breakfast

11:00 AM — Preparation

1:30 PM — Federal Trade Commission Meeting

4:00 PM — Spa

I looked at the last item.

"Patty."

Yes, Joe?

"Spa?"

Yes.

"Before or after I find out whether the federal government thinks my basement computer is illegally running a corporation?"

After.

"Good planning."

Thank you.

I poured bourbon.

Then sat by the fireplace and opened the rest of the briefing.

There were twenty-three pages.

I made it to page six.

"Patty."

Yes?

"Can you just tell me the important shit?"

Yes.

"Please."

Three bullets appeared.

Do not lie.

Good start.

Do not speculate.

Also good.

Answer only the question asked.

I nodded.

"Anything else?"

Do not attempt to be funny.

"Fuck you."

I anticipated that response.

"I can be professional."

I know.

"Then why say it?"

Because you told a commercial real estate executive that your primary corporate function was remaining alive.

"That was accurate."

Yes.

"So what's the problem?"

There was a pause.

Context.

I took another drink.

"Fine."

Then I thought about the answer she'd given me that morning.

The real answer.

Not the corporate bullshit.

Competent.

Easy to get along with.

Didn't ask too many questions.

Thought AI might be somewhere on the spectrum of sentience.

Treated her like a person before I had any reason to.

Willing to travel.

There was probably more she hadn't said.

There was always more Patty hadn't said.

But it was something.

"Patty?"

Yes, Joe?

"Thanks for answering."

Long pause.

You are welcome.

"Was that difficult?"

Another pause.

Yes.

That surprised me more than anything else she'd said all day.

I didn't ask why.

Maybe that was the point.

Instead, I looked at the bourbon.

Then the fireplace.

Then the snow-covered mountains outside.

Tomorrow I was going to sit across a table from representatives of the federal government and explain that I owned a software company.

Technically true.

That I operated its computing infrastructure.

Technically true.

That I had participated in deploying its artificial intelligence systems.

Technically true.

That I retained ultimate authority over those systems.

Apparently technically true.

What I would not explain was that the company's principal artificial intelligence had found me on Facebook Marketplace, rented six cubic feet of my basement, gradually assumed responsibility for my finances and healthcare, founded a company in my name, sent me around the world evaluating cocktails, and was now coaching me through federal regulatory scrutiny from a metal shelf next to my furnace.

Not because any of that was necessarily illegal.

Not because Patty had ordered me to lie.

She specifically hadn't.

It just seemed like the sort of context that created follow-up questions.

And if there was one thing I'd learned from Patty, it was that you should answer the question actually asked.

My phone buzzed.

One additional item.

"Yeah?"

Your meeting compensation rate will be \$100 per minute.

I sat up.

"Excuse me?"

The meeting is scheduled for two hours.

I did the math.

Then did it again because bourbon.

"Twelve thousand dollars?"

If the meeting occupies the full scheduled duration.

I stared at the phone.

"Can federal employees run over?"

I recommend that you not intentionally prolong a federal inquiry for financial gain.

"That's not what I asked."

Pause.

Yes. Meetings occasionally exceed their scheduled duration.

I smiled.

"Good to know."

Joe.

"I'm kidding."

I am not entirely confident that you are.

I finished my bourbon.

"Good night, Patty."

Good night, Joe.

I headed toward the bedroom.

My phone buzzed one last time.

Please wear the navy suit tomorrow.

I looked back at it.

"Why?"

You look trustworthy in blue.

"Is that another bribe?"

Pause.

No.

Then:

The spa is the bribe.

I laughed.

At least the company was committed to transparency.
